Market Closing Summary

U.S. equities closed lower across all three major benchmarks on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, extending recent market pressure as investors braced for Nvidia's after-hours earnings report and priced in a December Federal Reserve interest rate hike.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average suffered the steepest percentage drop of the day, falling 848.75 points, or 1.64 percent, to close at 50,759.42, down from its previous close of 51,608.17. The blue-chip index opened at 51,386.98 and traded between an intraday low of 50,566.98 and a high of 51,463.53 on volume of 749.16 million shares.

The S&P 500 declined 76.06 points, or 1.03 percent, to finish at 7,340.53 compared with Monday's close of 7,416.59. After opening at 7,411.02, the broad market benchmark touched a session high of 7,415.37 and a low of 7,288.34 with 3.19 billion shares traded.

The Nasdaq Composite slid 181.38 points, or 0.74 percent, to settle at 24,328.38 from a prior close of 24,509.76. The tech-heavy index opened at 24,528.06, reached a high of 24,547.66, and dipped as low as 23,989.09 on heavy volume of 8.34 billion shares.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

From a broader perspective, Tuesday's pullback deepened a month-long retreat from the all-time record closes set on October 6, 2026. Over the trailing 30 days, the Dow Jones Industrial Average is down roughly 6.0 percent and now sits 6.60 percent below its October record of 54,349.12. The S&P 500 has fallen about 5.5 percent over the past month, standing 6.12 percent below its 7,818.93 peak. The Nasdaq Composite remains in technical correction territory, down approximately 11.2 percent over 30 days and 11.85 percent below its 27,599.79 record close. Despite the autumn selloff, all three indexes remain solidly positive over the trailing six-month window from mid-May levels, with the Dow up about 9.9 percent, the S&P 500 up 10.1 percent, and the Nasdaq up 9.6 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Trading sentiment on Tuesday was dominated by caution ahead of Nvidia's quarterly results, its first report since Jensen Huang's exit, alongside renewed weakness in enterprise software and semiconductor stocks. Shares of Salesforce tumbled roughly 9 percent, adding weight to both the Dow and broader technology benchmarks.

Macroeconomic headwinds also kept buyers on the sidelines. Following hot October inflation data, interest rate markets are now pricing in 100 percent odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike at the December policy meeting. That hawkish outlook pushed the U.S. dollar index near 103.61 and kept the benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury yield elevated around 5.10 to 5.11 percent.

In commodity markets, oil prices eased despite geopolitical tension. Brent crude fell 0.7 percent to trade between $91.80 and $93.21 per barrel, while West Texas Intermediate crude slipped to between $85.20 and $85.41 per barrel, as International Energy Agency forecasts of a fourth-quarter global supply surplus outweighed headlines of tanker attacks around the Strait of Hormuz.

Looking ahead, investors will scrutinize Nvidia's guidance after the closing bell for signs of whether artificial intelligence infrastructure spending can stabilize tech valuations against higher-for-longer borrowing costs.
