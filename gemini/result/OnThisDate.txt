In 1850, California was formally admitted to the Union as the thirty-first United States state as a central component of the Compromise of 1850. Driven by the transformative California Gold Rush of 1849, which rapidly surged the region's population and commercial activity, California entered the Union directly as a free state without first undergoing a transitional territorial period. The historic compromise, brokered by Senators Henry Clay and Daniel Webster, established California's state borders stretching across the Pacific coastline, profoundly reshaping American continental politics, transcontinental transport corridors, and Pacific trade.


In 1947, computer scientists and naval researchers working on the Harvard Mark II electromechanical computer, under the direction of pioneer Grace Hopper, discovered and documented the world's first literal computer bug. While investigating an operational failure in the massive room-sized computing system at Harvard University, technician William Bill Burke located a moth trapped between the contacts of Relay number 70 in Panel F. The team carefully extracted the insect, taped it into their official engineering logbook, and noted: First actual case of bug being found. This historic entry popularized the terms bug and debugging throughout modern software engineering and computational sciences.
