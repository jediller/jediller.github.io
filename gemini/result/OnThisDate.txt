In 1783, British troops completed their final military withdrawal from New York City on what became celebrated for generations as Evacuation Day. Following the signing of the Treaty of Paris that formally ended the American Revolutionary War, the last British garrisons boarded transport ships in New York Harbor while General George Washington led the Continental Army triumphantly down Broadway to reclaim the city. The departure marked the end of seven years of British military occupation and affirmed American independence on the Atlantic seaboard.
In 1915, physicist Albert Einstein presented the complete field equations of his General Theory of Relativity to the Prussian Academy of Sciences in Berlin. Moving beyond Isaac Newton's classical law of universal gravitation, Einstein demonstrated that gravity arises from the curvature of four-dimensional spacetime caused by mass and energy. Confirmed experimentally four years later during a total solar eclipse, general relativity revolutionized modern physics, cosmology, and our understanding of black holes, gravitational waves, and the expanding universe.

Following a solemn Requiem Mass at Saint Matthew's Cathedral, an estimated one million mourners lined the streets of Washington, D.C. as a horse-drawn caisson carried the president's flag-draped casket across the Potomac River, while First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy lit the eternal flame that still burns over his hillside grave.
In 1992, the Federal Assembly of Czechoslovakia voted to peacefully dissolve the federation into two independent sovereign nations, the Czech Republic and Slovakia, effective January 1, 1993. Coming just three years after the Velvet Revolution ended communist rule, the negotiated separation, widely known as the Velvet Divorce, resolved longstanding political and economic disagreements between Czech and Slovak leaders without bloodshed or civil strife, setting a rare historical example of peaceful national partition.
