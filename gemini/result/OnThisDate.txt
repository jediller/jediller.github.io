In 1860, Abraham Lincoln was elected the sixteenth President of the United States, securing victory in a historic four-way presidential election that fundamentally transformed the trajectory of the nation. Running as the candidate of the relatively young Republican Party on a platform opposing the westward expansion of slavery, Lincoln defeated Northern Democrat Stephen A. Douglas, Southern Democrat John C. Breckinridge, and Constitutional Union candidate John Bell. Although Lincoln won only forty percent of the national popular vote and was omitted from ballots across ten southern states, his decisive sweep of the populous northern and western states secured an absolute majority in the Electoral College. His historic election served as the immediate catalyst for southern secession, beginning with South Carolina the following month, and set the stage for the American Civil War and the eventual abolition of slavery.

Played on a field with twenty-five players per side using modified rules resembling soccer and rugby, the historic match featured round balls that could only be kicked or batted rather than carried or thrown. Rutgers emerged victorious by a final score of six goals to four, inaugurating the rich tradition of American collegiate football.

Originally created and hosted by pioneering journalist Martha Rountree alongside Lawrence E. Spivak, the program transitioned from radio to television to provide in-depth journalistic questioning of prominent world leaders, cabinet members, and public officials, becoming the longest-running program in the history of American broadcasting.
