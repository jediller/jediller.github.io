In 1861, the First Transcontinental Telegraph line across North America was completed in Salt Lake City, Utah, connecting eastbound lines from California with westbound lines from Nebraska. The historic connection established instantaneous electrical telegraphic communication between the Atlantic and Pacific coasts of the United States. The inaugural message was sent by California Chief Justice Stephen Field to President Abraham Lincoln, expressing loyalty to the Union. The dramatic achievement reduced coast-to-coast message times from ten days to mere seconds, rendering the Pony Express obsolete.
In 1901, sixty-three-year-old schoolteacher Annie Edson Taylor achieved international fame by becoming the first person to successfully plunge over Niagara Falls in a barrel and survive. Taylor designed a custom oak barrel reinforced with iron hoops and padded with a heavy canvas mattress. On her birthday, she was sealed inside the barrel and cast into the raging rapids of the Niagara River above Horseshoe Falls, plummeting one hundred and sixty-seven feet into the turbulent basin below. Rescued alive with minor bruises, Taylor's daring feat inaugurated the era of Niagara daredevils.
In 1929, Wall Street was engulfed by unprecedented selling on Black Thursday, marking the opening shock of the Wall Street Crash of 1929. Panic seized the floor of the New York Stock Exchange as an unprecedented twelve million nine hundred thousand shares traded in a single session, overwhelming ticker-tape systems and erasing billions in valuation. Despite efforts by leading bankers to stabilize prices, Black Thursday shattered public confidence and signaled the onset of the Great Depression.
