In 1789, New Jersey became the very first state in the newly formed United States to ratify the Bill of Rights. Proposed by the First Federal Congress earlier that autumn under the leadership of James Madison, the twelve original constitutional amendments were designed to safeguard fundamental individual liberties, including freedom of speech, religion, and the press, as well as protections against unreasonable searches and cruel punishments. By ratifying eleven of the twelve articles, New Jersey set the constitutional ratification process in motion, culminating two years later when Virginia's approval made the first ten amendments the supreme law of the land.
In 1910, Mexican reformer and opposition leader Francisco Madero issued the Plan de San Luis Potosi, calling on citizens to rise up in armed revolt against the three-decade authoritarian rule of President Porfirio Diaz. Denouncing fraudulent elections and demanding democratic governance and land reform, Madero's manifesto ignited the Mexican Revolution. Over the ensuing decade, the sweeping social and political upheaval transformed Mexican society, toppled the old regime, and produced the landmark Constitution of 1917.
In 1945, the historic Nuremberg trials opened at the Palace of Justice in Nuremberg, Germany, as an International Military Tribunal convened by the Allied powers began prosecuting twenty-four senior Nazi leaders for war crimes, crimes against peace, and crimes against humanity. Presided over by judges from the United States, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, and France, the proceedings introduced the principle that national leaders and officials could be held personally accountable under international law for atrocities, establishing the foundational precedent for modern international criminal justice.
In 1998, the Zarya control module lifted off aboard a Proton rocket from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan, becoming the first component of the International Space Station placed into orbit. Built in Russia under an American contract, Zarya provided initial electrical power, propulsion, and guidance for the fledgling orbital outpost. Over the following decades, international astronauts and cosmonauts expanded the station into a permanent microgravity research laboratory symbolizing global scientific cooperation in low Earth orbit.
