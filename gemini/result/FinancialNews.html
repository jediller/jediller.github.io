Market Closing Summary

United States stocks finished mixed on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, as renewed selling across artificial intelligence and semiconductor shares dragged the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 lower, while strength in retail and defensive names lifted the Dow Jones Industrial Average back above 50,000.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 423.39 points, or 0.85 percent, to close at 50,018.70, building on Monday's finish of 49,595.31. After opening lower at 49,494.60 and dipping to an early low of 49,294.49, the blue-chip index rallied to an intraday high of 50,075.12 on volume of 712.75 million shares.

The S&P 500 slipped 36.08 points, or 0.50 percent, to settle at 7,201.09 compared with Monday's close of 7,237.17, marking its eighth loss in the last nine trading sessions. Opening at 7,172.33, the broad market benchmark moved between a low of 7,138.93 and a high of 7,223.95 with 3.30 billion shares traded.

The Nasdaq Composite declined 356.02 points, or 1.48 percent, to close at 23,733.54 from Monday's level of 24,089.56. The tech-heavy index opened at 23,745.86 and ranged between a high of 23,884.74 and a low of 23,455.84 on volume of 7.90 billion shares.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

Looking at the broader historical picture, all three major averages remain well below their October 6, 2026 all-time record closes. Over the trailing 30 days, the Dow Jones Industrial Average has fallen roughly 5.4 percent and sits 7.97 percent below its record close of 54,349.12. The S&P 500 is down approximately 7.2 percent over the past month, standing 7.90 percent below its 7,818.93 peak. The Nasdaq Composite remains in technical correction territory, down about 11.1 percent over 30 days and 14.01 percent beneath its 27,599.79 record high. Over the trailing six-month window from mid-May levels, all three benchmarks still retain solid gains, with the Dow up roughly 8.3 percent, the S&P 500 up 8.0 percent, and the Nasdaq up 6.9 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Tuesday's divergence reflected a pullback in high-flying technology stocks after Monday's relief rally, as Nvidia, AMD, Micron, and Anthropic retreated amid persistent skepticism over artificial intelligence spending returns and expectations for a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December. In contrast, retail shares including Dick's Sporting Goods, Kohl's, and Best Buy climbed on quarterly earnings, helping power the Dow higher.

In bond and commodity markets, the benchmark 10-year United States Treasury yield eased to around 4.92 to 4.93 percent, hovering near a two-week low after softer November flash purchasing managers' index readings. Meanwhile, crude oil prices traded lower, with Brent near $89 per barrel and West Texas Intermediate around $79 to $80 per barrel, as investors awaited Wednesday's Personal Consumption Expenditures inflation report and third-quarter gross domestic product update ahead of Thursday's Thanksgiving holiday closure.
