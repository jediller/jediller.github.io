Market Closing Summary

United States equities finished mixed on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, as the Nasdaq Composite edged higher while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell more than 560 points and the S&P 500 logged its fifth consecutive daily decline.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average led losses among the major benchmarks, dropping 561.25 points, or 1.11 percent, to close at 50,198.17, down from Tuesday's finish of 50,759.42. The blue-chip index opened at 50,627.36 and traded between an intraday high of 50,637.70 and a low of 50,029.35 on volume of 691.47 million shares.

The S&P 500 slipped 16.90 points, or 0.23 percent, to settle at 7,323.63 compared with its prior close of 7,340.53, marking its longest losing streak since June. After opening at 7,355.69, the broad market index moved between a high of 7,367.68 and a low of 7,285.76 with 3.06 billion shares traded.

Bucking the broader weakness, the Nasdaq Composite gained 101.50 points, or 0.42 percent, to finish at 24,429.88, up from Tuesday's close of 24,328.38. The tech-heavy index opened at 24,486.17 and ranged between a low of 24,225.30 and a high of 24,538.78 on volume of 6.97 billion shares.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

Looking at the broader trend, all three benchmarks remain well below their October 6, 2026 all-time record closes. Over the past 30 days, the Dow Jones Industrial Average has declined roughly 4.4 percent and now sits 7.64 percent below its record close of 54,349.12. The S&P 500 is down approximately 5.0 percent over the trailing month, standing 6.33 percent below its 7,818.93 peak. The Nasdaq Composite remains in technical correction territory, down about 7.5 percent over 30 days and 11.49 percent beneath its 27,599.79 record high. Even after this autumn pullback, all three averages preserve solid gains over the trailing six-month period from mid-May levels, with the Dow up about 8.7 percent, the S&P 500 up 9.8 percent, and the Nasdaq up 10.0 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Market movement on Wednesday centered on Nvidia, which initially rallied after reporting stronger-than-expected third-quarter earnings and revenue, before reversing lower during the session amid investor caution over artificial intelligence valuations and tighter United States chip export rules. Traders also watched Anthropic ahead of its initial public offering pricing tonight under the ticker ANT.

In bond and currency markets, the benchmark 10-year United States Treasury yield eased roughly five to six basis points to around 5.06 to 5.07 percent as falling oil prices tempered inflation anxiety, even as Federal Reserve meeting minutes reinforced expectations for tighter monetary policy and lifted the United States dollar to multi-month highs.

In commodity trading, crude oil prices retreated on rising United States crude inventories and ample global supply expectations. Brent crude traded near $93 to $94 per barrel, down from early highs, while West Texas Intermediate crude also softened, helping ease pressure on Treasury yields as investors looked ahead to Thursday's retail earnings from Walmart.
