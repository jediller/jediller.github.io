Market Closing Summary

United States equities suffered a sharp reversal and broad selloff on Thursday, November 19, 2026, as an early morning rally faded and the S&P 500 tumbled to its sixth consecutive daily loss, its longest losing streak since 2024, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average plunged more than 1,100 points.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 1,101.11 points, or 2.19 percent, to close at 49,097.06, down from Wednesday's finish of 50,198.17. After opening higher at 50,432.32 and reaching an early peak of 50,454.81, the blue-chip index slid to an intraday low of 49,091.92 on volume of 729.41 million shares.

The S&P 500 fell 147.80 points, or 2.02 percent, to settle at 7,175.83 compared with its prior close of 7,323.63. The broad market benchmark opened at 7,389.26 and touched an early high of 7,415.64 before reversing down to a low of 7,173.63 on volume of 3.34 billion shares.

The Nasdaq Composite posted the steepest percentage drop of the three major averages, tumbling 550.69 points, or 2.25 percent, to close at 23,879.20 from Wednesday's level of 24,429.88. After surging at the open to 24,809.05 and hitting a session high of 24,939.62, the tech-heavy index sank to a low of 23,870.70 on heavy volume of 8.15 billion shares.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

Thursday's selloff widened each benchmark's retreat from the all-time record closes set on October 6, 2026. Over the trailing 30 days, the Dow Jones Industrial Average has fallen roughly 6.5 percent and now sits 9.66 percent below its record close of 54,349.12, approaching correction territory. The S&P 500 is down approximately 7.0 percent over the past month, standing 8.22 percent below its 7,818.93 peak. The Nasdaq Composite remains firmly in a technical correction, down about 9.6 percent over 30 days and 13.48 percent beneath its 27,599.79 record high. Over the trailing six-month window from mid-May levels, all three indexes still hold positive gains, with the Dow up about 6.3 percent, the S&P 500 up 7.6 percent, and the Nasdaq up 7.6 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Market volatility centered on Anthropic's landmark Nasdaq trading debut under the ticker ANT after pricing its initial public offering at $83 per share to raise more than $81 billion at a roughly $1.5 trillion valuation. While early enthusiasm lifted technology futures, the initial rally evaporated as investors weighed lofty artificial intelligence valuations, cautious retail guidance from Walmart, and an unexpected rise in weekly initial jobless claims to a ten-week high of 203,000.

In fixed income and currency markets, the benchmark 10-year United States Treasury yield eased slightly to around 5.05 to 5.07 percent on safe-haven bond demand, even as hawkish Federal Reserve minutes reinforced expectations for a December interest rate hike and pushed the United States dollar higher.

In commodity trading, crude oil prices weakened on global supply surplus data despite ongoing Middle East tensions, with Brent crude settling near $92 per barrel and West Texas Intermediate crude trading in the low $80s per barrel.
