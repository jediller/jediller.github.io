Market Closing Summary

United States stocks suffered a steep, technology-led selloff on Friday, November 20, 2026, pushing both the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average into correction territory and erasing the Nasdaq Composite's gains for the year.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points, or 1.93 percent, to close at 48,147.33, down from Thursday's finish of 49,097.06. After touching an early high of 49,594.90 from an open of 49,071.82, the blue-chip average slid to an intraday low of 47,998.86 on heavy volume of 1.17 billion shares.

The S&P 500 tumbled 265.44 points, or 3.70 percent, to finish at 6,910.39 compared with its prior close of 7,175.83, marking its seventh consecutive daily loss. The broad market benchmark opened at 7,150.39 and traded between a high of 7,198.67 and a low of 6,882.66 with 4.73 billion shares changing hands.

The Nasdaq Composite bore the brunt of the decline, plunging 1,261.47 points, or 5.28 percent, to settle at 22,617.73 from Thursday's close of 23,879.20, turning negative for 2026. Opening at 23,592.67, the tech-heavy index touched a session peak of 23,837.76 before dropping to a low of 22,513.01 on massive volume of 10.42 billion shares.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

Friday's rout placed all three major benchmarks squarely in technical correction territory, defined as a drop of at least 10 percent from the all-time record closes set on October 6, 2026. Over the trailing 30 days, the Dow Jones Industrial Average has fallen roughly 8.9 percent and now sits 11.41 percent below its record close of 54,349.12. The S&P 500 is down approximately 10.9 percent over the past month, standing 11.62 percent below its 7,818.93 peak. The Nasdaq Composite has dropped about 15.3 percent over 30 days and now sits 18.05 percent beneath its 27,599.79 record high. Across the trailing six-month window from mid-May levels, gains have narrowed sharply, leaving the Dow up roughly 4.2 percent, the S&P 500 up 3.6 percent, and the Nasdaq up 1.9 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Market selling accelerated on monthly options expiration day as Anthropic shares slumped following Thursday's record $93.5 billion initial public offering, dragging down major semiconductor and artificial intelligence names including Nvidia, AMD, Micron, and Broadcom amid mounting doubts over valuation and capital spending durability, while defensive money rotated toward consumer staples.

In the bond market, a flight to safety sent Treasury prices higher and pushed the benchmark 10-year United States Treasury yield down 13 basis points to finish at 4.92 percent, breaking back below the 5 percent threshold while the 2-year yield ended at 4.63 percent, even as futures continued pricing in high odds of a December Federal Reserve rate hike. Meanwhile, crude oil prices remained subdued in the mid-$80s for West Texas Intermediate and low-$90s for Brent ahead of next week's Thanksgiving holiday.
