Weekend Market Status & Friday Close

United States financial markets are closed today for the weekend, following a turbulent week that ended with a steep, technology-led selloff on Friday, November 20, 2026. Using the most recent official Friday closing benchmarks, all three major United States stock indexes finished the week in technical correction territory, with the Nasdaq Composite turning negative for the year.

At Friday's closing bell, the Dow Jones Industrial Average stood at 48,147.33 after dropping 949.73 points, or 1.93 percent, on the session from Thursday's close of 49,097.06. Friday's intraday trading ranged between a high of 49,594.90 and a low of 47,998.86 on heavy volume of 1.17 billion shares.

The S&P 500 finished Friday at 6,910.39, falling 265.44 points, or 3.70 percent, from its prior close of 7,175.83 and logging its seventh consecutive daily loss. The broad market benchmark moved between a session high of 7,198.67 and a low of 6,882.66 on volume of 4.73 billion shares.

The Nasdaq Composite ended Friday at 22,617.73, plunging 1,261.47 points, or 5.28 percent, from Thursday's close of 23,879.20. After opening at 23,592.67 and touching a high of 23,837.76, the tech-heavy index sank to a session low of 22,513.01 on massive volume of 10.42 billion shares.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

From a broader historical perspective, Friday's official closes confirm that all three major averages are now more than 10 percent below their October 6, 2026 all-time record highs. Over the trailing 30-day period, the Dow Jones Industrial Average has declined roughly 8.9 percent and sits 11.41 percent below its record close of 54,349.12. The S&P 500 has fallen approximately 10.9 percent over the past month, standing 11.62 percent below its 7,818.93 peak. The Nasdaq Composite has dropped about 15.3 percent over 30 days and sits 18.05 percent beneath its 27,599.79 record close. Over the trailing six-month window from mid-May levels, earlier gains have narrowed substantially, leaving the Dow up roughly 4.2 percent, the S&P 500 up 3.6 percent, and the Nasdaq up 1.9 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Investor anxiety during the closing session centered on a sharp pullback in artificial intelligence and semiconductor stocks after Anthropic shares fell following Thursday's record $93.5 billion initial public offering. Major chipmakers including Nvidia, AMD, Micron, and Broadcom saw heavy selling on monthly options expiration day as traders questioned capital spending returns, while defensive money rotated into consumer staples.

In fixed income and commodities, safe-haven demand pushed the benchmark 10-year United States Treasury yield down 13 basis points on Friday to finish at 4.92 percent, dipping back below 5 percent while the 2-year yield closed at 4.63 percent, even as futures markets continued pricing in strong odds of a December Federal Reserve rate hike. Meanwhile, crude oil prices remained subdued in the mid-$80s for West Texas Intermediate and low-$90s for Brent ahead of the holiday-shortened Thanksgiving trading week.
