Movers and Shakers for Thursday, November 26, 2026.
President Donald Trump and Vice President J.D. Vance addressed two major domestic and national security developments on Wednesday ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. Speaking on the controlled detonation of lead azide in Iowa, President Trump praised the Army's mitigation efforts, saying they far exceeded expectations. Environmental Protection Agency Administrator Lee Zeldin reinforced that message on social media platform X, reporting that EPA monitors showed zero airborne lead five hours after the detonation and noting that Kansas City's World Cup FanFest fireworks released roughly ten times more particulate matter. Meanwhile, Vice President Vance credited White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller for making tough decisions and averting potential disaster, while also downplaying any need for a presidential pardon for Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang after President Trump remarked that a pardon is always an option.
Lawmakers and industry leaders offered sharp reactions to the escalating scrutiny surrounding Nvidia's chip exports and the administration's latest trade policy moves. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer strongly criticized Huang over allegations of shipping advanced chips to China, calling the exports the equivalent of selling rocket engines to the Soviets during the space race and describing the situation as an unfathomable failure of enforcement. Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich echoed concerns on X, writing that there was something profoundly wrong about Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy. On trade, incoming Senate Finance Committee Chair Ron Wyden blasted the administration's new round of Section 301 tariffs on Wednesday, stating that Donald Trump lacks object permanence and noting that only weeks after Americans overwhelmingly voted against his high tariffs and higher prices agenda, he is back with another round. Wyden pledged to restore Congress's constitutional authority over trade. Dan Anthony, executive director of the coalition We Pay the Tariffs, similarly urged Congress to end the tariffs, warning that customers cannot afford more price increases and small businesses cannot afford to absorb tariffs on top of tariffs either.
On the foreign policy front, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent commented on escalating U.S. economic pressure against Tehran as the Treasury Department sanctioned two dozen Iranian individuals and shadow banking entities. Bessent declared that Iran's economy is underwater and its leaders are panicking as Washington continues to tighten financial restrictions alongside ongoing maritime tensions.
