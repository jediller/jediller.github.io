Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Tennessee and the Nordic kingdom of Norway.
Tennessee, celebrated as the Volunteer State, is a landlocked state in the Southeastern United States spanning roughly forty-two thousand square miles and bordering eight neighboring states. Extending over four hundred miles from the Appalachian Mountains in the east to the Mississippi River in the west, Tennessee is legally and geographically divided into three distinct Grand Divisions: East, Middle, and West Tennessee. East Tennessee is dominated by the rugged Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains, crowned by Clingmans Dome at six thousand six hundred and forty-three feet, alongside the fertile Tennessee Valley. Middle Tennessee centers on the rolling Highland Rim and the limestone-rich Nashville Basin, home to the state capital. West Tennessee descends across the Gulf Coastal Plain toward the alluvial bluffs of Memphis overlooking the Mississippi River. Economically and culturally, Tennessee blends global musical heritage in Nashville and Memphis with powerhouse automotive manufacturing, healthcare management, logistics centered around Memphis air cargo hubs, and diverse agriculture in soybeans, cotton, and beef cattle.
Norway, officially the Kingdom of Norway, occupies the western and northern portions of the Scandinavian Peninsula in Northern Europe, bordering Sweden, Finland, and Russia while facing the North Sea, the Norwegian Sea, and the Barents Sea. Encompassing approximately one hundred and forty-eight thousand square miles including the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard, Norway features one of the most dramatic glaciated coastlines on Earth. Carved by ancient ice sheets, its western shore is indented by thousands of deep, steep-walled sea fjords such as the Sognefjord and Geirangerfjord, framed by the rugged Scandinavian Mountains rising to Galdhopiggen at eight thousand one hundred feet. Extending well north of the Arctic Circle into the Land of the Midnight Sun, Norway enjoys a surprisingly temperate coastal climate warmed by the North Atlantic Current. Economically, Norway leverages vast North Sea offshore oil and natural gas reserves, abundant hydroelectricity that powers over ninety percent of its domestic grid, world-leading salmon aquaculture, timber forestry, and international maritime shipping anchored in Oslo and Bergen.
