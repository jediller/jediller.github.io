Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Georgia and the Mediterranean nation of Greece.


Georgia, known as the Peach State, is located in the southeastern United States. Covering roughly fifty-nine thousand square miles, Georgia is the largest state east of the Mississippi River by land area. It borders Tennessee and North Carolina to the north, South Carolina and the Atlantic Ocean to the east, Florida to the south, and Alabama to the west. Georgia spans five distinct provinces: the Blue Ridge Mountains in the northeast, home to Brasstown Bald at four thousand seven hundred and eighty-four feet; the Ridge and Valley province; the Cumberland Plateau; the rolling Piedmont plateau in the center; and the expansive Coastal Plain in the south, featuring the Okefenokee Swamp. Georgia's fall line separates the Piedmont from the Coastal Plain along rivers like the Savannah and Chattahoochee. Economically, Georgia leverages its strategic geography to support a dynamic economy anchored by the Atlanta metropolitan area, a global hub for aviation, logistics, and technology. The state leads the nation in peanuts, pecans, blueberries, and timber, while the deepwater container port of Savannah ranks among the busiest maritime gateways in North America.


Greece, officially the Hellenic Republic, occupies the southern tip of the Balkan Peninsula in Southeastern Europe at the crossroads of Europe, Asia, and Africa. Covering roughly fifty-one thousand square miles, Greece is bounded by the Aegean, Ionian, and Mediterranean seas, bordering Albania, North Macedonia, Bulgaria, and Turkey. The physical geography of Greece is exceptionally mountainous, with rugged terrain covering approximately eighty percent of the mainland, culminating in Mount Olympus at nine thousand five hundred and seventy feet. Greece also encompasses several thousand islands, including Crete, Rhodes, and the Cyclades, giving it an extraordinary coastline stretching over eight thousand five hundred miles. Despite scarce arable land, Greece has leveraged its maritime geography into a global economic power, controlling the world's largest merchant shipping fleet by tonnage. Tourism represents another vital pillar of the economy, driven by historical archaeology in Athens and picturesque islands, alongside agricultural exports of olives, olive oil, feta cheese, and wine.
