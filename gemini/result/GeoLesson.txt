Today's United States location is Wisconsin. Situated in the Upper Midwestern region of the United States, Wisconsin is bounded by Lake Superior and Upper Michigan to the north, Lake Michigan to the east, Illinois to the south, and Iowa and Minnesota across the Mississippi and Saint Croix rivers to the west. Known as America's Dairyland, Wisconsin covers over fifty-four thousand square miles and features physical geography heavily shaped by ancient continental glaciation. Its landforms transition from the northern Superior Upland, characterized by dense boreal forests, glacial kettle lakes, and the Apostle Islands, to the unglaciated Driftless Area in the southwest, featuring deeply carved river bluffs and valleys, and the fertile rolling till plains of the southeast.


Wisconsin's physical terrain supports a robust agricultural, manufacturing, and shipping economy. Its eastern coastline along Lake Michigan hosts major industrial port cities including Milwaukee, Green Bay, and Kenosha, which serve as heavy manufacturing, beer brewing, and Great Lakes maritime commerce hubs. Rich glacial soils make Wisconsin a national leader in dairy production, cheese manufacturing, cranberries, and ginseng. Madison, situated on an isthmus between Lake Mendota and Lake Monona in south-central Wisconsin, serves as the state capital and a major university and biotechnology research center.


Today's country is Kenya. Located on the eastern coast of Africa straddling the equator, Kenya borders Ethiopia to the north, Somalia to the northeast, South Sudan to the northwest, Uganda to the west, Tanzania to the south, and the Indian Ocean to the southeast. Covering over two hundred twenty-four thousand square miles, Kenya features diverse physical geography ranging from the warm coastal lowlands along the Indian Ocean to the vast elevated central plateau divided by the Great Rift Valley, culminating in snow-capped Mount Kenya, the second highest peak in Africa.


Kenya's unique geography shapes its ecological and economic identity. The fertile volcanic soils of the central highlands surrounding Nairobi support intensive tea, coffee, and fresh flower cultivation, making Kenya a leading global agricultural exporter. The Great Rift Valley contains major alkaline and freshwater lakes, while the southern savannas, including the famed Maasai Mara, host extensive wildlife reserves that drive world-renowned ecotourism. Mombasa, located on the Indian Ocean coast, features a major natural deepwater harbor serving as the primary maritime trade gateway for East and Central Africa.
