Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Texas and the North American nation of Canada.


Texas, known as the Lone Star State, is the second-largest state in the United States by both area and population, spanning over two hundred sixty-eight thousand square miles in the South Central region. Its vast landscape is divided into four major physical regions: the Gulf Coastal Plains in the east and south, featuring dense pine forests, bayous, and major port cities like Houston and Corpus Christi; the North Central Plains, characterized by rolling hills, prairies, and fertile agricultural land; the Great Plains in the north and northwest, including the expansive Panhandle and the rugged Llano Estacado plateau; and the Basin and Range province of the Trans-Pecos in the west, home to the Guadalupe Mountains and Big Bend National Park along the Rio Grande. The state's climate ranges from humid subtropical in the east to arid desert in the far west. Economically, Texas is a global powerhouse, leading the nation in crude oil and natural gas production, wind energy capacity, and cattle ranching, while hosting major international centers for commercial aerospace, telecommunications, semiconductor manufacturing, and biomedical research centered in Dallas, Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, and Houston.


Canada is the world's second-largest country by total area, encompassing nearly three point eight five million square miles across the northern half of North America, extending from the Atlantic to the Pacific and northward into the Arctic Ocean. The country's geography is defined by several vast physiographic regions: the Canadian Shield, a massive geological core of ancient crystalline rock, boreal forest, and countless freshwater lakes that wraps around Hudson Bay; the fertile St. Lawrence Lowlands and Great Lakes basin in the southeast, which forms the demographic and industrial heartland; the expansive interior Canadian Prairies of Manitoba, Saskatchewan, and Alberta, renowned for grain agriculture and energy resources; the rugged Western Cordillera, dominated by the majestic Canadian Rockies and coastal mountain ranges in British Columbia; and the vast, fragile Arctic Archipelago in the far north. Canada boasts the world's longest coastline and holds an estimated twenty percent of the planet's surface freshwater. Economically, Canada is a highly developed market economy possessing immense wealth in natural resources, including petroleum, natural gas, timber, uranium, gold, and nickel, complemented by world-class automotive manufacturing, aerospace engineering, software technology, and financial services in global cities such as Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver, and Calgary.
