Egypt's geography is overwhelmingly hyper-arid desert, dominated by the Sahara and Eastern Deserts, with over ninety-five percent of its population living along the narrow, fertile ribbon of the Nile River valley and delta.


Egypt's geography is defined by the Nile River, which provides virtually all usable fresh water and fertile alluvial soils. The Suez Canal, cutting through the Isthmus of Suez, connects the Mediterranean and Red seas, serving as one of the world's most critical maritime shipping chokepoints and driving international commercial maritime trade.
