Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the southeastern United States state of Tennessee and the Nordic maritime kingdom of Norway.
Tennessee, celebrated as the Volunteer State, is situated in the southeastern United States. Encompassing roughly forty-two thousand square miles, Tennessee stretches over four hundred and thirty miles from the Appalachian Mountains to the Mississippi River, bordered by eight states. Physically and culturally, the state is divided into three Grand Divisions: East, Middle, and West Tennessee. East Tennessee is dominated by the Blue Ridge Mountains and Great Smoky Mountains National Park, crowned by Clingmans Dome at six thousand six hundred and forty-three feet. Moving westward across the Cumberland Plateau and Central Basin surrounding Nashville, the terrain transitions into Middle Tennessee's rolling limestone hills. West Tennessee slopes gently across the Gulf Coastal Plain to the bluffs overlooking the Mississippi River at Memphis. Drained by the Tennessee and Cumberland River systems, the state's geography catalyzed historic hydroelectric development under the Tennessee Valley Authority. Economically, Tennessee combines agriculture in soybeans, cattle, and cotton with powerhouse manufacturing in automotive assembly, healthcare administration, aerospace research at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and global logistics centered on Memphis.
Norway, officially the Kingdom of Norway, occupies the western and northern portion of the Scandinavian Peninsula in Northern Europe, stretching along the North Atlantic and Arctic Oceans. Covering approximately one hundred and forty-nine thousand square miles, Norway features one of the most rugged coastlines on Earth, spanning over fifty-eight thousand miles when accounting for thousands of islands, rocky skerries, and monumental glacial fjords such as Geirangerfjord and Naeroyfjord. The interior is dominated by the Scandinavian Mountains, crowned by Galdhopiggen at eight thousand one hundred feet in the Jotunheimen range. Extending well above the Arctic Circle into the land of the Midnight Sun, Norway exhibits diverse climates moderated by the warm North Atlantic Current. Strategically controlling North Atlantic maritime gateways, Norway has transformed its geography into extraordinary prosperity. Leveraging offshore petroleum and natural gas fields in the North Sea, Norway manages the world's largest sovereign wealth fund, while generating over ninety percent of its domestic electricity from clean hydroelectric cascades, leading the globe in electric vehicle adoption and sustainable shipping.
