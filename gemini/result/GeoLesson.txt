Washington, celebrated as the Evergreen State, is situated in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. Covering approximately seventy-one thousand square miles, Washington is bordered by the Canadian province of British Columbia to the north, Idaho to the east, Oregon to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The state's physical geography is sharply divided into two distinct regions by the towering Cascade Range. Western Washington is characterized by temperate marine rainforests, the deep saltwater fjord-like waterways of Puget Sound, and the Olympic Mountains on the Olympic Peninsula. Stratovolcanoes dominate the Cascades, crowned by Mount Rainier, an active glaciated peak rising to fourteen thousand four hundred and eleven feet, alongside Mount Saint Helens and Mount Baker. East of the Cascades, the terrain transitions into the semi-arid Columbia Plateau, featuring extensive basalt lava plains, rolling wheat fields in the Palouse, and fertile river valleys irrigated by the massive Columbia River. Economically, Washington harnesses the Columbia River for the nation's largest hydroelectric power generation, driving an advanced economy in aerospace engineering, cloud computing, digital technology centered in Seattle, commercial fruit and apple orchards, and international maritime container shipping.


Portugal, officially the Portuguese Republic, occupies the southwestern portion of the Iberian Peninsula in southwestern Europe, bounded by Spain to the north and east and the North Atlantic Ocean to the west and south. Spanning roughly thirty-five thousand square miles, Portugal also includes the autonomous volcanic archipelagos of the Azores and Madeira in the North Atlantic. Portugal's continental geography is bisected by the Tagus River, creating distinct physical zones: the mountainous, green northern plateau characterized by granite highlands, deep river gorges like the Douro Valley, and dense pine and eucalyptus woodlands; and the rolling plains and golden lowlands of the southern Alentejo and coastal Algarve regions. With over one thousand miles of Atlantic coastline, Portugal's identity has been linked to the sea for millennia, launching the Age of Discovery. Economically, Portugal is the world's leading producer and exporter of natural cork, while its fertile river basins sustain world-renowned viticulture, olive oil production, and maritime fisheries. In modern industry, Portugal has become a global leader in renewable energy generation, drawing over seventy percent of its electricity from advanced wind, solar, and hydroelectric networks, complemented by thriving tech hubs and historic cultural tourism in Lisbon and Porto.
