Located in the southeastern South Central region of the United States, Arkansas is bordered by Missouri to the north, Tennessee and Mississippi to the east across the Mississippi River, Louisiana to the south, and Texas and Oklahoma to the west. Arkansas's diverse physical terrain supports vital agricultural, manufacturing, logistics, and mineral industries. Little Rock, centrally situated along the Arkansas River, serves as the state capital and economic hub, while northwestern Arkansas serves as a premier global retail corporate management, technology, and transportation corridor.

It is bordered by Slovakia to the north, Ukraine to the northeast, Romania to the east, Serbia and Croatia to the south, Slovenia to the southwest, and Austria to the west. Covering approximately thirty-six thousand square miles, Hungary's topography is dominated by the Great Hungarian Plain, known as the Alföld, stretching across the east, the rolling hills and low mountains of Transdanubia in the west, and Lake Balaton, Central Europe's largest freshwater lake.


Hungary's geography is defined by two major European waterways: the Danube River, which bisects the country from north to south, and the Tisza River in the east. The capital city of Budapest, formed by the union of historic Buda on the western hills and Pest on the eastern plain, serves as a primary financial, industrial, and transportation hub. Renowned for its abundant subterranean thermal mineral springs, Hungary combines rich agricultural production of grains, sunflowers, and viticulture with advanced automotive manufacturing, electronics, pharmaceuticals, and software technology sectors that integrate it deeply into the European economy.
