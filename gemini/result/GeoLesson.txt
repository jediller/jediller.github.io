Today's United States location is North Dakota. Situated in the Upper Midwestern region of the United States along the Canadian border, North Dakota is bounded by Manitoba and Saskatchewan to the north, Minnesota across the Red River of the North to the east, South Dakota to the south, and Montana to the west. Covering over seventy thousand square miles, North Dakota features physical geography divided into three main landforms: the fertile Red River Valley in the east, the rolling Drift Prairie in the central region, and the elevated Great Plains and Missouri Plateau in the west, featuring the rugged North Dakota Badlands carved by the Little Missouri River.


North Dakota's geography underpins an economy focused on energy production, agriculture, and logistics. The fertile soils of the Red River Valley make the state a national leader in spring wheat, durum wheat, sunflowers, canola, and barley production. In western North Dakota, the Bakken Formation within the Williston Basin has established the state as one of the nation's leading producers of crude oil and natural gas. Bismarck, located on the Missouri River in central North Dakota, serves as the state capital, while Fargo, on the eastern border, acts as a major commercial, healthcare, and educational hub.

Dense boreal forests cover nearly three-quarters of the country's land area.


Finland's northern geography shapes its environmental and economic character. One-third of Finland lies north of the Arctic Circle in Lapland, experiencing the midnight sun in summer and polar night in winter. Finland's vast forest reserves drive world-leading forestry, pulp, and paper industries, while its coastal cities, including the capital Helsinki on the Gulf of Finland, support advanced technology, clean energy research, shipbuilding, and Baltic maritime commerce.
