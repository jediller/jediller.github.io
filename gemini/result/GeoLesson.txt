Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Idaho and the East Asian nation of South Korea.
Idaho, celebrated as the Gem State, is situated in the Pacific Northwest and Mountain West regions of the United States. Spanning roughly eighty-three thousand square miles as the fourteenth-largest state by land area, Idaho exhibits a dramatic, mountainous topography defined by the northern reaches of the Rocky Mountains. The state's northern panhandle is carpeted with dense evergreen forests, glacial lakes like Lake Pend Oreille and Coeur d'Alene, and deep river valleys that border Canada. In central Idaho, the formidable, wilderness-carved Sawtooth, Bitterroot, and Salmon River mountain ranges form one of the largest contiguous roadless wilderness areas in the continental United States, cleaved by the raging Salmon River, known as the River of No Return. In the south, the terrain transforms dramatically across the broad, sweeping arc of the Snake River Plain, an ancient volcanic basalt depression carved by the Snake River, featuring dramatic geological wonders such as Shoshone Falls and Hells Canyon, the deepest river gorge in North America. Economically and culturally, Idaho pairs its world-famous specialty agriculture, leading the nation in commercial potato production alongside barley and dairy farming, with vast timber harvesting, historical silver and phosphate mining, and a rapidly expanding semiconductor manufacturing and technology hub centered in the capital city of Boise.
South Korea, officially the Republic of Korea, occupies the southern half of the mountainous Korean Peninsula in East Asia, encompassing approximately thirty-eight thousand square miles between the Yellow Sea to the west and the Sea of Japan to the east, separated from North Korea by the heavily fortified Demilitarized Zone. Nearly seventy percent of the country is ruggedly mountainous, dominated along its eastern spine by the Taebaek Mountains, which slope steeply toward the eastern coast while descending more gently toward fertile agricultural lowlands, broad river deltas, and deeply indented archipelagos in the west and south. In the south, subtropical Jeju Island hosts Hallasan, an extinct shield volcano that forms the nation's highest natural elevation at six thousand three hundred and ninety-eight feet. South Korea has transformed its mountainous geography and maritime positioning into an economic miracle, evolving into one of the world's preeminent high-technology and industrial powerhouses. The nation commands global leadership in semiconductor fabrication, consumer electronics, commercial shipbuilding, and automotive manufacturing, anchored by the sprawling metropolitan capital region of Seoul along the Han River and the bustling maritime deep-water port of Busan.
