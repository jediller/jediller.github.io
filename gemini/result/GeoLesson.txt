Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Alaska and the republic of South Africa.


Alaska, known as The Last Frontier, is by far the largest state in the United States by land area, spanning more than six hundred and sixty-three thousand square miles—an area more than twice the size of Texas. Situated in the extreme northwest of North America, Alaska is bordered by Canada to the east, the Arctic Ocean to the north, and the Pacific Ocean and Bering Sea to the south and west. Its geography is defined by colossal physical features: the Pacific mountain system, featuring the towering Alaska Range crowned by Denali, the highest peak in North America at twenty thousand three hundred ten feet; the vast interior basin drained by the mighty Yukon River; and the rugged Brooks Range sloping into the Arctic tundra of the North Slope. The state also encompasses the Aleutian Islands volcanic archipelago and the glacier-carved fjords of the Alexander Archipelago in the southeast Panhandle, home to the capital city of Juneau. Economically, Alaska is a global powerhouse in energy extraction from Prudhoe Bay, commercial marine fishing—leading the nation in wild salmon, pollock, and king crab—mineral mining including zinc and gold, and eco-tourism centered in Anchorage and coastal waterways.


South Africa is located at the southernmost tip of the African continent, covering more than four hundred and seventy thousand square miles, bordered by Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and Eswatini, while completely encircling the kingdom of Lesotho. Its diverse coastline stretches over one thousand seven hundred miles along both the South Atlantic and Indian oceans, meeting at the Cape of Good Hope and Cape Agulhas. The geography of South Africa is anchored by a vast inland plateau known as the Highveld, rimmed by the dramatic Great Escarpment, which reaches its highest elevations in the rugged Drakensberg mountains. Below the escarpment lie fertile coastal lowlands, the semi-arid Karoo plateau, and the renowned Mediterranean climate region of the Western Cape. Economically, South Africa is Africa's most industrialized economy and a global mining leader, possessing the world's largest reserves of platinum group metals, manganese, chromium, and substantial gold and diamond resources. Complemented by major manufacturing hubs in Johannesburg, world-class agricultural exports including citrus and wine from the Western Cape, and key maritime shipping ports in Durban and Cape Town, South Africa serves as a critical economic gateway for the African continent.
