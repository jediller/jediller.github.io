Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Wyoming and the Central American nation of Costa Rica.


Wyoming, known as the Equality State, is located in the Mountain West region of the United States. It is the tenth largest state by land area, spanning nearly ninety-eight thousand square miles, yet it ranks as the least populous state in the nation. The geography of Wyoming is characterized by the convergence of the Great Plains with the rugged Rocky Mountains. The eastern portion of the state comprises the High Plains, featuring vast open grasslands and plateaus. Moving westward, the topography rises dramatically into prominent mountain ranges, including the Bighorn Mountains, the Wind River Range, and the iconic Teton Range. Northwestern Wyoming is home to Yellowstone National Park, the world's first national park, renowned for its geothermal geysers, hot springs, and diverse wildlife ecosystems. The state's climate is predominantly semi-arid and continental, with cold winters and warm summers. Economically, Wyoming is a powerhouse in mineral extraction, leading the nation in coal production and possessing substantial reserves of trona, natural gas, crude oil, and uranium, alongside robust livestock ranching and outdoor tourism sectors.


Costa Rica is a vibrant Central American country bordered by Nicaragua to the north, Panama to the southeast, the Caribbean Sea to the east, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Spanning approximately nineteen thousand seven hundred square miles, Costa Rica possesses an extraordinary geographical and ecological diversity. The country's interior is dominated by a central volcanic mountain spine comprising the Cordillera de Guanacaste, Cordillera Central, and Cordillera de Talamanca, where the highest peak, Cerro Chirripó, rises to over twelve thousand five hundred feet. These highlands are flanked by lush Caribbean coastal lowlands and diverse Pacific coastal plains, ranging from tropical dry forests in the northwest to dense rainforests on the Osa Peninsula. Costa Rica is globally celebrated for its environmental stewardship, with more than twenty-five percent of its landmass protected within national parks and biological reserves. The national capital, San José, is situated in the fertile Central Valley, the country's demographic and economic core. Costa Rica's economy thrives on advanced medical technology manufacturing, software services, ecotourism, and high-quality agricultural exports including coffee and bananas.
