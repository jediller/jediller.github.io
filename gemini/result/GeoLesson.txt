Georgia, known as the Peach State and the Empire State of the South, is located in the southeastern region of the United States. Spanning roughly fifty-nine thousand square miles, Georgia is the largest state by land area east of the Mississippi River. The state encompasses diverse physiographic provinces that descend from north to south: the rugged Blue Ridge Mountains, where the Appalachian Trail begins at Springer Mountain; the Ridge and Valley province; the rolling red clay hills of the Piedmont, which forms the demographic and commercial heartland surrounding the capital city of Atlanta; and the expansive Atlantic and Gulf Coastal Plains in the south. The Fall Line, where rivers cascade over rapids, historically marked the inland limit of commercial river navigation and spurred the growth of cities like Augusta, Macon, and Columbus. Georgia's coastal plain terminates in a rich network of barrier islands known as the Golden Isles, while the deepwater Port of Savannah serves as one of the fastest-growing container shipping gateways in North America. Economically, Georgia combines agricultural leadership in peanuts, pecans, poultry, blueberries, and timber with an economic powerhouse anchored by global logistics, aviation, automotive manufacturing, software development, and a premier film and television production hub.


Norway, officially the Kingdom of Norway, is a Northern European nation occupying the western and northern portion of the Scandinavian Peninsula. Covering roughly one hundred and forty-nine thousand square miles, including the Arctic archipelagos of Svalbard and Jan Mayen, Norway possesses one of the most dramatically fractured coastlines on Earth, stretching over fifty-one thousand miles when including its thousands of islands and deep glacial fjords. The Scandinavian Mountains form the spine of the country, crowned by Galdhøpiggen at eight thousand one hundred feet, and give rise to massive icefields. Carved by ancient glaciers, world-famous fjords such as Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord cut deeply into vertical coastal mountain walls. Over a third of Norway lies north of the Arctic Circle, experiencing the midnight sun in summer and the polar night in winter, though warm waters from the North Atlantic Current keep coastal ports ice-free year-round. Economically, Norway leverages its geography with remarkable sophistication: the nation is a major exporter of petroleum and natural gas from the North Sea, which fuels the world's largest sovereign wealth fund. Norway also generates over ninety-five percent of its electricity from clean hydroelectric sources, while sustaining world-leading maritime shipping, Atlantic salmon aquaculture, and eco-tourism centered around Oslo, Bergen, and Tromsø.
