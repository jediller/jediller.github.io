Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the Upper Midwestern United States state of Minnesota and the South American Pacific nation of Peru.
Minnesota, celebrated as the Land of Ten Thousand Lakes and the North Star State, is situated in the Upper Midwest region of the United States. Encompassing roughly eighty-seven thousand square miles, Minnesota is bordered by the Canadian provinces of Ontario and Manitoba to the north, Lake Superior and Wisconsin to the east, Iowa to the south, and South Dakota and North Dakota to the west. Shaped profoundly by repeated continental glaciations, Minnesota's physical landscape features four distinct ecological zones: the prairie grasslands of the southwest, the fertile deciduous Big Woods of the central corridor, the vast aspen parklands of the northwest, and the boreal wilderness of the northeast. In the northeast, the ancient Canadian Shield terrain hosts the Superior Upland, the dense forests of the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness, and the rich iron-bearing formations of the Mesabi Iron Range, which still produces the vast majority of the nation's iron ore. At Lake Itasca in northwestern Minnesota, the mighty Mississippi River begins its two-thousand-three-hundred-mile journey to the Gulf of Mexico. Economically, Minnesota pairs premier agricultural production in corn, soybeans, sugar beets, and turkeys with global leadership in medical technology, retail logistics, and freshwater commerce anchored by the Twin Cities metropolitan area and the international port of Duluth on Lake Superior.
Peru, officially the Republic of Peru, occupies a premier geographical position along the western Pacific coastline of South America, spanning approximately four hundred and ninety-six thousand square miles as the third-largest nation on the continent. Peru's geography is famously partitioned into three sharply contrasting natural regions: the Costa, the Sierra, and the Selva. The Costa is a narrow, arid coastal desert ribbon cooled by the nutrient-dense Humboldt Current, which supports one of the richest marine fisheries on Earth. Rising dramatically behind the coast are the towering Andes Mountains, or Sierra, crowned by the glaciated peak of Mount Huascaran at twenty-two thousand two hundred and five feet, alongside the high Altiplano plateau surrounding Lake Titicaca and the historic valleys of Cusco and the Sacred Valley of the Incas. East of the Andean crest, the terrain plunges into the Selva, a vast Amazon Basin rainforest covering over sixty percent of Peru's territory and drained by the headwaters of the mighty Amazon River system. Leveraging this extraordinary geographic and mineral diversity, Peru is a global titan in mining, leading the world in copper, silver, and zinc extraction, complemented by booming agricultural exports in asparagus, avocados, and specialty coffee.
