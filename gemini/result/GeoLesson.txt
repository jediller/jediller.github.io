Vermont, known as the Green Mountain State, is located in the New England region of the northeastern United States. Spanning approximately nine thousand six hundred square miles, Vermont is the second least populous American state and the only New England state without an Atlantic coastline. It is bordered by New York to the west, Massachusetts to the south, New Hampshire across the Connecticut River to the east, and Quebec to the north. Vermont's physical geography is dominated by the Green Mountains, a scenic range of the Appalachian chain that includes Mount Mansfield, the state's highest peak at four thousand three hundred and ninety-three feet. Along its western flank lies the Lake Champlain Valley, a fertile agricultural lowland and major freshwater basin shared with New York and Canada. Economically, Vermont leverages its pastoral landscape to support artisanal agriculture, famously leading the nation in pure maple syrup production, alongside dairy farming, specialty cheeses, and craft brewing. The state is also a historic center for stone quarrying, producing marble and granite in Barre and Proctor, while ski resorts drive outdoor recreation. Montpelier serves as the state capital, while Burlington anchors commercial technology, healthcare, and higher education.


South Korea, officially the Republic of Korea, occupies the southern half of the Korean Peninsula in East Asia, extending southward between the Yellow Sea to the west and the Sea of Japan, or East Sea, to the east, separated from Japan by the Korea Strait. Covering roughly thirty-eight thousand seven hundred square miles, South Korea possesses a mountainous terrain covering approximately seventy percent of the country, dominated by the Taebaek Mountain Range along the eastern coast. Coastal plains and fertile river valleys in the west and south, drained by the Han and Nakdong rivers, host the majority of the population and intensive agriculture. South Korea also encompasses thousands of coastal islands, led by subtropical Jeju Island, home to Mount Hallasan, the country's highest peak at six thousand three hundred and ninety-eight feet. Despite scarce domestic mineral resources, South Korea has transformed its geography into an export-oriented economic powerhouse, standing as the world's thirteenth-largest economy. The capital city of Seoul anchors a metropolitan region housing nearly half the population. South Korea is a global titan in semiconductor fabrication, memory chips, automotive engineering, commercial shipbuilding, and electric vehicle batteries, supported by deepwater container ports in Busan and Incheon.
