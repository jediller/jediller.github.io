Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Washington and the transcontinental nation of Egypt.
Washington, known as the Evergreen State, occupies the northwestern corner of the contiguous United States, bordered by the Canadian province of British Columbia to the north, Idaho to the east, Oregon along the Columbia River to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Covering roughly seventy-one thousand three hundred square miles, Washington is dramatically divided by the north-south Cascade Range, crowned by glaciated volcanic Mount Rainier at fourteen thousand four hundred and eleven feet. West of the Cascades, the Puget Sound lowland and the rugged Olympic Mountains on the Olympic Peninsula experience a mild, wet marine climate supporting lush temperate rainforests and the major metropolitan ports of Seattle and Tacoma. East of the mountain crest, a pronounced rain shadow creates the dry, sunny Columbia Basin around Spokane and Tri-Cities, where the Columbia and Snake rivers irrigate vast orchards, wheat fields, and vineyards. Washington's deep-water Pacific ports and abundant hydroelectric power fuel an economy led by software and cloud technology, commercial aerospace, maritime shipping, agriculture, and forestry.
Egypt, officially the Arab Republic of Egypt, sits in the northeastern corner of Africa while extending across the Sinai Peninsula into southwestern Asia, bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, the Red Sea to the east, Sudan to the south, and Libya to the west. Spanning roughly three hundred and eighty-seven thousand square miles, Egypt is overwhelmingly arid desert, including the Western Desert and Eastern Desert, yet its human geography is concentrated along the fertile Nile River Valley, Lake Nasser behind the Aswan High Dam, and the fan-shaped Nile Delta. Flowing northward into the Mediterranean, the Nile sustains major urban centers including Cairo and Alexandria within a narrow ribbon of irrigated farmland. At Egypt's northeastern crossroads, the man-made Suez Canal links the Mediterranean and Red Seas, handling a vital share of global maritime trade. Egypt's strategic geography and ancient heritage anchor an economy driven by Suez Canal transit revenues, natural gas, agriculture, manufacturing, and international tourism.
