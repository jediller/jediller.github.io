In 1943, graduate student Albert Schatz, working in the laboratory of microbiologist Selman Waksman at Rutgers University in New Jersey, successfully isolated streptomycin, the first effective antibiotic discovered against tuberculosis. Prior to Schatz's discovery, tuberculosis, commonly known as the white plague, was a catastrophic global affliction with no known pharmacological cure, resulting in widespread fatalities and prolonged sanitarium quarantines. Isolated from the soil microbe Streptomyces griseus, streptomycin demonstrated potent bactericidal activity against Mycobacterium tuberculosis and gram-negative bacteria. The landmark discovery revolutionized infectious disease treatment, saving millions of lives worldwide and earning Waksman the 1952 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Rising to twenty-six thousand eight hundred and sixty-four feet along the border between Nepal and Tibet, Cho Oyu ranks as the sixth-highest mountain in the world. Ascending via the northwest face without supplemental oxygen, the team demonstrated that lightweight alpine-style techniques could succeed on the planet's highest peaks, marking a monumental milestone in twentieth-century high-altitude mountaineering.
