In 1907, Oklahoma was officially admitted to the Union as the forty-sixth United States state through an official proclamation signed by President Theodore Roosevelt, uniting the Oklahoma Territory and the Indian Territory into a single commonwealth. Known as the Sooner State, Oklahoma's rapid transition to statehood followed the famous land runs of the late nineteenth century and booming oil discoveries near Tulsa, quickly establishing the new state as a vital center of American agriculture, cattle ranching, aerospace, and domestic petroleum and natural gas production.
In 1914, the twelve regional Federal Reserve Banks officially opened for business across the United States, putting the newly enacted Federal Reserve Act into full operation. Created in response to the severe financial panic of 1907 to provide the nation with a safer, more flexible, and more stable monetary and banking system, the Federal Reserve System transformed American central banking, regulating credit conditions, supervising member banks, and anchoring modern national economic policy through dual congressional mandates for maximum employment and long-term price stability.
In 1933, the United States and the Soviet Union formally established diplomatic relations following sixteen years of non-recognition after the 1917 Bolshevik Revolution. Negotiated in Washington by President Franklin Delano Roosevelt and Soviet Foreign Commissar Maxim Litvinov, the historic agreement opened embassies in Moscow and Washington, laying the diplomatic groundwork for bilateral trade, formal ambassadorial exchanges, and eventual World War Two wartime alliance cooperation against the Axis powers.
In 1945, representatives of thirty-seven countries meeting in London signed the constitution establishing the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization, widely known internationally as UNESCO. Born from the conviction that lasting international peace must be built upon intellectual and moral solidarity, the agency has championed global literacy, scientific collaboration, and the international preservation of irreplaceable World Heritage cultural and natural sites across more than one hundred and sixty member nations worldwide.
