Washington, known as the Evergreen State, is located in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. Covering roughly seventy-one thousand square miles, Washington is renowned for its remarkable geographic contrasts, sharply divided from north to south by the towering Cascade Range. To the west of the Cascades lies a maritime climate characterized by the Puget Sound lowlands, deep saltwater fjords, and the rugged Olympic Peninsula, which hosts temperate rainforests that receive over one hundred and forty inches of rainfall annually. Dominating the skyline is Mount Rainier, an active, glacier-clad stratovolcano rising to fourteen thousand four hundred and ten feet. East of the Cascades, the rain shadow creates a semi-arid landscape across the Columbia Plateau, where extensive irrigation from the Columbia River basin nourishes world-class agriculture, producing the nation's leading harvests of apples, sweet cherries, pears, hops, and premium wine grapes. Economically, Washington leverages its Pacific Rim geography as a major international maritime shipping gateway and global titan in aerospace manufacturing, software engineering, cloud computing, and retail commerce centered around Seattle, Tacoma, Bellevue, and Spokane.


Armenia, officially the Republic of Armenia, is a landlocked nation situated in the South Caucasus region of Eurasia, located at the cultural and geographic crossroads of Eastern Europe and Western Asia. Encompassing roughly eleven thousand five hundred square miles, Armenia celebrates its National Day today, September 21, commemorating its 1991 independence. The country's terrain is intensely mountainous and situated within the Armenian Highland, with an average elevation exceeding five thousand nine hundred feet. The highest peak is Mount Aragats, an extinct volcano rising to thirteen thousand four hundred and nineteen feet, while the iconic snow-capped peak of Mount Ararat forms a breathtaking visual backdrop just across the western border. A central physical feature is Lake Sevan, one of the largest high-altitude freshwater alpine lakes in the world, covering nearly five hundred square miles and providing vital hydroelectric power, irrigation, and fisheries. Armenia's semi-arid, continental climate fosters fertile agriculture across the Ararat plain, celebrated for apricots, grapes, and world-renowned brandy production. Strategically positioned along historic Silk Road trading routes, Armenia is rapidly developing a high-tech software and engineering economy centered around its ancient capital of Yerevan.
