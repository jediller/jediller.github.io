Today's United States location is Montana. Situated in the Mountain West region of the northern United States, Montana is bounded by Canada to the north, North Dakota and South Dakota to the east, Wyoming to the south, and Idaho to the west. Montana features a dramatic geographical divide: the eastern two-thirds of the state consists of broad, rolling Great Plains prairies and river valleys, while the western third is dominated by rugged, snow-capped ranges of the Rocky Mountains. Major physical landmarks include Glacier National Park, Flathead Lake, and the headwaters of the Missouri and Columbia river systems.


Montana's geography significantly shapes its economy and culture. Vast eastern grasslands support cattle ranching and grain farming, while rich mineral deposits drive copper and coal mining. Western alpine mountains and pristine wilderness areas foster a thriving forestry, recreation, and outdoor tourism industry.


Today's country is Greece. Located in Southeastern Europe on the southern tip of the Balkan Peninsula, Greece is bordered by Albania, North Macedonia, and Bulgaria to the north, Turkey to the east, the Aegean Sea to the east, the Ionian Sea to the west, and the Mediterranean Sea to the south. Greece's geography is intensely fragmented and mountainous, featuring over two thousand islands, of which roughly two hundred are inhabited, including Crete, Rhodes, and Corfu.


Greece's mountain ranges cover nearly eighty percent of the land, limiting large-scale inland agriculture. However, its extensive, deeply indented coastline—the longest in the Mediterranean basin—has made maritime navigation, commercial shipping, island transport, seafood harvesting, and historical global tourism central pillars of the national economy.
