Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Texas and the European nation of Switzerland.
Texas, known as the Lone Star State, is the second-largest state in the United States by both area and population, covering roughly two hundred and sixty-eight thousand six hundred square miles across the South Central region. Bordered by Oklahoma to the north, Arkansas and Louisiana to the east, New Mexico to the west, Mexico along the Rio Grande to the south, and the Gulf of Mexico to the southeast, Texas spans four major physiographic regions. The humid Gulf Coastal Plains stretch inland from barrier islands to the Piney Woods and the Blackland Prairies supporting Houston, Dallas, and San Antonio. Central Texas rises into the limestone karst hills of the Edwards Plateau and the Great Plains of the Panhandle, while far West Texas features the arid Chihuahuan Desert and the rugged Guadalupe Mountains, peaking at Guadalupe Peak at eight thousand seven hundred and fifty-one feet. This vast geographic diversity drives a powerhouse economy anchored by oil and natural gas production, wind and solar energy, aerospace, semiconductor manufacturing, cattle ranching, and international Gulf Coast port trade.
Switzerland, officially the Swiss Confederation, is a small, mountainous, landlocked country in the heart of Western and Central Europe, bordered by Germany to the north, Austria and Liechtenstein to the east, Italy to the south, and France to the west. Spanning roughly fifteen thousand nine hundred square miles, Switzerland divides into three distinct natural regions. The rugged Swiss Alps cover the southern and eastern two-thirds of the country, rising to fifteen thousand two hundred and three feet at Dufourspitze and feeding major European waterways including the Rhine and Rhone rivers. To the north, the Jura Mountains form a forested limestone arc along the French border. Between these two mountain chains lies the densely populated Swiss Plateau, a rolling landscape of glacial lakes such as Lake Geneva and Lake Zurich where the capital of Bern, financial hub Zurich, and diplomatic Geneva are located. Switzerland's alpine terrain and central crossroads location fostered centuries of armed neutrality and a high-value economy built on global banking, pharmaceuticals, precision watchmaking, hydroelectric power, and alpine tourism.
