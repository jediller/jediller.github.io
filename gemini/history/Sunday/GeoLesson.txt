Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Maine and the Central American nation of Panama.


Maine, known as the Pine Tree State, is the northernmost and easternmost state in the New England region of the United States. Spanning approximately thirty-five thousand three hundred square miles, it is the largest state in New England by land area yet one of the least densely populated. Maine's geography is defined by its rugged Atlantic coastline, featuring thousands of offshore islands, tidal bays, and granite headlands, including the iconic landscape of Acadia National Park on Mount Desert Island. Inland, the topography ascends into the Longfellow Mountains, the northern extension of the Appalachian chain, where Mount Katahdin marks the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail. The state's interior is dominated by the North Maine Woods, a vast expanse of boreal and northern hardwood forests covering more than eighty percent of the state. Economically, Maine is globally celebrated for its commercial marine fisheries, leading the nation in lobster harvesting, alongside specialty blueberry agriculture, paper and timber manufacturing, naval shipbuilding at Bath Iron Works, and outdoor recreation centered around its lakes, rivers, and coastal waters.


Panama is a transcontinental nation situated on the narrow isthmus connecting North and South America. Bordered by Costa Rica to the west, Colombia to the southeast, the Caribbean Sea to the north, and the Pacific Ocean to the south, Panama covers approximately twenty-nine thousand square miles. The country's topography is anchored by a central mountain spine, the Cordillera Central, which slopes downward into lush tropical coastal lowlands, mangrove estuaries, and extensive rainforest watersheds. The southeastern border region features the Darién Gap, a dense, undeveloped wilderness area separating Central America from the South American mainland. Panama's geographic position makes it one of the most critical logistical hubs on the planet. The Panama Canal, an engineering marvel spanning fifty miles between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans, serves as a primary artery for global maritime trade. Panama City, the nation's capital, is a dynamic international financial, banking, and trade hub, while the country's economy is further driven by maritime port operations, air transit logistics, copper mining, and international commerce.
