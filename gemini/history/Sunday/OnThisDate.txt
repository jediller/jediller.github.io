In 1895, German physicist Wilhelm Conrad Rontgen made one of the most momentous discoveries in scientific history when he accidentally detected an entirely new, invisible form of electromagnetic radiation at the University of Wurzburg. Working with a shielded cathode-ray tube, Rontgen observed that a nearby fluorescent screen glowed with green light, revealing unknown rays passing directly through cardboard and air. Naming his discovery X-rays, Rontgen captured the first medical radiograph depicting the skeletal bones and ring of his wife's hand, earning him the inaugural 1901 Nobel Prize in Physics and revolutionizing global medical diagnostics and radiation physics.

Known as the Treasure State and Big Sky Country, Montana's journey to statehood culminated decades of rapid development following territorial establishment in 1864. Defending civil liberties and fair housing, Brooke broke a decades-long color barrier in Congress four years after becoming the first African American state attorney general. In the Senate, Brooke co-authored the landmark Fair Housing Act of 1968, advancing nationwide equal opportunity.
In 1939, Swabian carpenter Georg Elser carried out a solo assassination attempt against Adolf Hitler at the Burgerbraukeller in Munich. Having spent months hollowing out a stone pillar behind the speaker's lectern to conceal a handmade timer bomb, Elser sought to halt the impending horrors of World War Two. Although the bomb detonated on schedule, Hitler had cut his speech short and departed thirteen minutes earlier, leaving Elser's resistance as a remarkable near-miss in modern European history.
