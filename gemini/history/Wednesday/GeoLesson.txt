Texas, known as the Lone Star State, is the second-largest state in the United States by both area and population, spanning approximately two hundred sixty-eight thousand square miles in the South Central region of the country. It is bordered by Oklahoma to the north, Arkansas and Louisiana to the east, the Gulf of Mexico to the southeast, Mexico to the south and southwest across the Rio Grande, and New Mexico to the west. The physical geography of Texas is exceptionally diverse, divided into four major regions: the Gulf Coastal Plains, featuring subtropical wetlands, pine forests, and major deepwater ports in Houston and Corpus Christi; the North Central Plains, characterized by rolling prairie and ranching country; the Great Plains of the Texas Panhandle and Hill Country, anchored by cattle grazing and wind energy; and the Basin and Range province of the Trans-Pecos in the far west, home to the rugged Guadalupe Mountains and Guadalupe Peak at eight thousand seven hundred fifty-one feet. Economically, Texas is an economic powerhouse with the eighth-largest economy in the world if it were a sovereign nation. It leads the United States in crude oil and natural gas production centered in the Permian Basin, petrochemical refining, wind and solar power generation, cattle and cotton agriculture, and global technology and aerospace hubs across Austin, Dallas-Fort Worth, and Houston.


Germany is a federal parliamentary republic situated in Central Europe, bordered by nine neighboring nations: Denmark to the north, Poland and the Czech Republic to the east, Austria and Switzerland to the south, and France, Luxembourg, Belgium, and the Netherlands to the west, with coastlines on the North and Baltic seas. Covering approximately one hundred thirty-seven thousand square miles, Germany's topography descends in steps from the Bavarian Alps in the south through the forested Central German Uplands to the low-lying North European Plain. Major navigable river systems, notably the Rhine, the Danube, and the Elbe, have historically served as critical arteries for European trade and industrial development. Germany is the economic engine of Europe and the fourth-largest economy in the world, renowned globally for precision automotive manufacturing, industrial machinery, chemicals, optics, and green energy technology. Its polycentric urban network features major metropolitan centers including the federal capital of Berlin, the financial hub of Frankfurt, the industrial Ruhr valley, and southern technological powerhouses in Munich and Stuttgart.
