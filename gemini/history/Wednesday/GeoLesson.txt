Today's United States location is Rhode Island. Officially nicknamed the Ocean State, Rhode Island is situated in the southern portion of New England and is the smallest state in the nation by total area, encompassing approximately one thousand two hundred fourteen square miles. Despite its compact footprint, Rhode Island boasts over four hundred miles of tidal shoreline, shaped extensively by Narragansett Bay, an expansive estuary that cuts deeply into the eastern half of the state and contains more than thirty islands, including Aquidneck, Conanicut, and Prudence.


The physical geography of Rhode Island is divided into two primary physiographic regions: the flat, sandy Coastal Lowland in the east and south along the Atlantic Ocean and Narragansett Bay, and the gently rolling Western New England Upland, which rises to Jerimoth Hill near the Connecticut border. The capital and largest city, Providence, situated at the head of Narragansett Bay, has long served as a historic center of jewelry manufacturing, precision toolmaking, and higher education anchored by Brown University and the Rhode Island School of Design. Across the bay, Newport is internationally famed for its Gilded Age coastal mansions, world-class sailing regattas, and the Naval War College, while coastal communities sustain active commercial fishing, oyster aquaculture, and defense-related marine engineering.


Today's country is Croatia. Located in Southeastern Europe along the eastern shores of the Adriatic Sea, Croatia occupies a crescent-shaped territory bridging Central Europe, the Mediterranean, and the Balkans. It is bordered by Slovenia to the northwest, Hungary to the northeast, Serbia to the east, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Montenegro to the southeast, and the Adriatic Sea to the southwest. Covering approximately twenty-one thousand eight hundred fifty square miles, Croatia features three distinct geographical zones: the fertile plains and river valleys of the Pannonian Basin drained by the Sava and Drava rivers, the forested limestone mountain belt of the Dinaric Alps, and a dramatically indented Adriatic coastline featuring more than one thousand islands and islets.


Croatia's strategic maritime and riverine position makes it a crucial transport, trade, and tourism gateway. The capital and largest metropolitan center, Zagreb, located along the Sava River at the southern slopes of Mount Medvednica, is the nation's political, cultural, and industrial hub. The Mediterranean coastal region, encompassing Istria and Dalmatia with historic ports like Split, Rijeka, and Dubrovnik, is renowned for its Roman and Venetian architecture, olive groves, and maritime shipping. Croatia's modern economy is anchored by international tourism, advanced pharmaceuticals, electrical machinery, shipbuilding, chemical production, and food processing.
