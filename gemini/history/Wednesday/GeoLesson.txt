Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Oregon and the European nation of Portugal.
Oregon, known as the Beaver State, is located in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States, bordered by Washington to the north, Idaho to the east, Nevada and California to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Spanning roughly ninety-eight thousand square miles as the ninth-largest state by area, Oregon is defined by dramatic north-south physiographic belts. Along the coast, the rugged Oregon Coast Range meets a scenic shoreline of sea stacks and temperate rainforests. Inland lies the fertile Willamette Valley, drained by the Willamette River and home to Portland, Salem, and Eugene, where mild, moist winters and dry summers support thriving agriculture in hazelnuts, berries, grass seed, and wine grapes. Rising immediately east of the valley is the volcanic Cascade Range, crowned by Mount Hood at eleven thousand two hundred and forty-nine feet and Crater Lake, the deepest lake in the United States, formed inside a collapsed volcanic caldera. Beyond the Cascades, eastern Oregon transitions into the arid high desert of the Columbia Plateau and the Basin and Range province, bordered by the Snake River through Hells Canyon. Oregon's geography shapes an economy anchored by timber, precision manufacturing, semiconductor fabrication, hydropower along the Columbia River, and outdoor recreation.
Portugal, officially the Portuguese Republic, occupies the westernmost portion of the Iberian Peninsula in southwestern Europe, sharing its eastern and northern land border exclusively with Spain while facing the Atlantic Ocean to the west and south. Covering approximately thirty-five thousand six hundred square miles including the Atlantic archipelagos of the Azores and Madeira, mainland Portugal is divided by the Tagus River, which flows westward through the capital city of Lisbon into a vast natural harbor. Northern Portugal is mountainous and green, featuring granite plateaus, the Douro River valley famous for terraced port wine vineyards, and the Serra da Estrela mountain range rising to six thousand five hundred and thirty-nine feet at Torre. Southern Portugal transitions into the rolling plains of the Alentejo and the warm, sun-drenched coastal cliffs and beaches of the Algarve. Moderated by Atlantic ocean currents, Portugal enjoys a temperate Mediterranean climate with wet winters and dry, sunny summers. Strategically positioned at the crossroads of the Atlantic and Mediterranean sea lanes, Portugal's maritime geography fueled its historic Age of Discovery and continues to support shipping, renewable wind and solar energy, cork forestry, textiles, technology services, and international tourism.
