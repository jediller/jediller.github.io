In 1987, representatives of twenty-four nations and the European Economic Community signed the landmark Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer in Montreal, Canada. Formulated in response to alarming scientific evidence discovering an expanding seasonal ozone hole over Antarctica, the treaty established mandatory international phase-outs of chlorofluorocarbons, halons, and other ozone-depleting chemicals. Universally ratified by one hundred ninety-eight signatories, the Montreal Protocol is widely regarded by environmental historians as the most effective international environmental treaty ever implemented, successfully stabilizing and facilitating the gradual recovery of the Earth's stratospheric ozone layer.
