Colorado, known as the Centennial State because it joined the Union in 1876, is situated in the Mountain West region of the United States. Spanning roughly one hundred and four thousand square miles, it ranks as the eighth-largest state by land area. Colorado is celebrated for its extraordinary topographical variety, divided into three principal geographic realms: the eastern High Plains, which gently rise from rolling prairie farmland into the Front Range; the towering Rocky Mountains, which bisect the state from north to south and contain fifty-eight peaks exceeding fourteen thousand feet in elevation, crowned by Mount Elbert at fourteen thousand four hundred and forty feet; and the Colorado Plateau in the west, characterized by rugged red-rock canyons, mesas, and river valleys. The Continental Divide runs through the crest of the Rockies, dictating North American hydrological drainage. Major river systems originating in Colorado include the Colorado River, the Rio Grande, the Arkansas River, and the South Platte River. Economically, Colorado has evolved from historic mining and ranching into a vibrant modern powerhouse driven by aerospace engineering, telecommunications, biotechnology, renewable energy, and world-class alpine tourism and outdoor recreation centered around Denver, Colorado Springs, Boulder, and premier mountain resort communities.


Chile is a remarkably narrow and elongated nation located along the southwestern edge of South America, stretching more than two thousand six hundred miles between the high peaks of the Andes Mountains to the east and the Pacific Ocean to the west, with an average width of just one hundred and ten miles. Today, September 18, holds special significance as Chile's National Day, celebrating the establishment of its first government junta in 1810. Chile exhibits dramatic climatic and geographical diversity across distinct latitudinal zones: in the north lies the hyper-arid Atacama Desert, one of the driest places on Earth and home to leading astronomical observatories and rich mineral deposits; the fertile Central Valley surrounding the capital city of Santiago enjoys a Mediterranean climate that sustains major fruit agriculture, world-renowned vineyards, and the majority of the nation's population; and the southern Chilean Patagonia region features pristine temperate rainforests, glaciers, fjords, and alpine archipelagos. Geologically situated on the Pacific Ring of Fire, Chile experiences frequent tectonic and volcanic activity. Economically, Chile is the world's leading producer of copper and a major supplier of lithium, while also boasting a thriving export economy in fresh produce, wine, salmon aquaculture, and forestry products.
