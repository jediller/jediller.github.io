Georgia, celebrated as the Peach State and the Empire State of the South, is located in the southeastern United States. Encompassing roughly fifty-nine thousand square miles as the largest state east of the Mississippi River by land area, Georgia spans five distinct physiographic provinces: the Cumberland Plateau, Valley and Ridge, Blue Ridge Mountains, Piedmont, and Atlantic Coastal Plain. In the north, the Blue Ridge rises dramatically, crowned by Brasstown Bald at four thousand seven hundred and eighty-four feet, forming the southern terminus of the Appalachian Trail. The rolling Piedmont plateau houses the dynamic Atlanta metropolitan area, the economic engine of the Southeast. Moving southward across the Fall Line, the landscape transitions into the vast Coastal Plain, drained by major river systems including the Chattahoochee, Flint, and Savannah Rivers, leading to nearly one hundred miles of coastal barrier islands known as the Golden Isles. Economically, Georgia pairs dominant agricultural output in peanuts, pecans, blueberries, and poultry with powerhouse commercial aviation, global logistics centered on the Port of Savannah and Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, film production, and telecommunications.


Morocco, officially the Kingdom of Morocco, occupies a strategic geographic position in northwest Africa at the meeting point of the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea across the Strait of Gibraltar. Spanning approximately one hundred and seventy-two thousand square miles, Morocco features remarkably diverse topography dominated by the soaring Atlas Mountain system, which traverses the country from southwest to northeast. The High Atlas range features Toubkal, the highest peak in North Africa at thirteen thousand six hundred and seventy-one feet. To the north, the Rif Mountains hug the Mediterranean coast, while the fertile coastal plains and plateaus between the mountains and the Atlantic support the country's historic cities of Casablanca, Rabat, Fes, and Marrakech. Southeast of the Atlas Mountains, the terrain descends abruptly into the vast, hyper-arid Sahara Desert, characterized by rocky plateaus and shifting sand dunes. Economically and strategically, Morocco serves as a bridge between Africa and Europe, holding over seventy percent of the world's known phosphate reserves, which are vital for global agriculture, while rapidly expanding as a regional manufacturing and renewable energy leader through the Tanger Med transshipment megaport, automotive assembly, and the massive Noor Ouarzazate solar power complex.
