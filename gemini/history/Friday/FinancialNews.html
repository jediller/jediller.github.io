Market Closing Summary

United States stocks surged in a shortened Black Friday session on Friday, November 27, 2026, capping Wall Street's strongest week since April 2025 as buyers rushed back into technology and artificial intelligence shares following the Thanksgiving break. Major equity exchanges closed early at 1:00 PM Eastern, with bond markets closing at 2:00 PM Eastern.

At the early closing bell, the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 591.99 points, or 1.18 percent, to finish at 50,774.94, building on Wednesday's close of 50,182.95. Opening higher at 50,458.86, the blue-chip index moved between an intraday low of 50,398.29 and a high of 50,830.19 on holiday-light volume of 365.41 million shares, finishing the week up 5.46 percent.

The S&P 500 rose 114.71 points, or 1.60 percent, to settle at 7,297.49 compared with Wednesday's close of 7,182.78. After opening at 7,262.61, the broad market benchmark ranged from 7,211.46 to 7,304.10 on volume of 1.68 billion shares, locking in a 5.60 percent weekly advance.

The Nasdaq Composite led the rally, jumping 543.70 points, or 2.31 percent, to close at 24,129.50 from Wednesday's finish of 23,585.80. The tech-heavy index opened at 23,958.46, touched a low of 23,690.60, and reached a high of 24,149.09 on volume of 4.76 billion shares, ending the week up 6.68 percent.

30-Day, 6-Month & All-Time High Context

From a broader historical perspective, Friday's advance narrowed losses from recent peaks, though all three benchmarks remain below their all-time record closes. Over the trailing 30 days since October 28, the Dow Jones Industrial Average is down 3.24 percent and sits 6.58 percent below its August 5 record close of 54,349.12. The S&P 500 has declined 5.77 percent over the past 30 days, standing 6.67 percent below its October 6 peak of 7,818.93. The Nasdaq Composite remains in technical correction territory, down 9.12 percent over 30 days and 12.57 percent beneath its October 6 record high of 27,599.89. Over the trailing six-month window since May 27, the Dow has edged back into positive territory, up 0.26 percent, while the S&P 500 is down 2.96 percent and the Nasdaq is down 9.54 percent.

Key Drivers, Yields & Commodities

Technology and retail shares drove sentiment as the holiday shopping season kicked off. Anthropic jumped 11 percent to $91.88, Broadcom gained 3.8 percent, Amazon rose 2.1 percent, and Microsoft added 0.6 percent, while Nvidia slipped 1.7 percent. Among traditional retailers, Macy's rose 1.2 percent while Target finished nearly flat. The National Retail Federation projects November and December holiday sales will grow 4.2 percent year over year to surpass $1 trillion, even as 82 percent of surveyed shoppers adjust spending habits amid 3.8 percent inflation and elevated energy costs.

In bond and commodity markets, the benchmark 10-year United States Treasury yield rose to 4.94 percent from 4.85 percent late Wednesday. Meanwhile, Brent crude oil eased 0.9 percent to $90.84 per barrel. Regular trading hours resume on Monday, November 30, ahead of next week's November employment and manufacturing reports.
