North Carolina, celebrated as the Tar Heel State and the Old North State, is located in the southeastern region of the United States. Covering approximately fifty-four thousand square miles, North Carolina extends from the Atlantic Ocean westward across three distinct physiographic provinces: the Atlantic Coastal Plain, the Piedmont plateau, and the Appalachian Mountains. The Coastal Plain features nearly three hundred miles of ocean coastline shielded by the dynamic, shifting barrier islands of the Outer Banks, alongside extensive estuaries such as the Pamlico and Albemarle Sounds. Moving inland, the gently rolling Piedmont region houses the state's largest metropolitan concentrations and economic engines, including the Research Triangle of Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill, as well as the major national financial center of Charlotte. In the west, the Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains rise dramatically, crowned by Mount Mitchell at six thousand six hundred and eighty-four feet, the highest peak in mainland North America east of the Mississippi River. Economically, North Carolina pairs traditional agricultural prominence in sweet potatoes, tobacco, and swine with world-class biotechnology, pharmaceutical manufacturing, banking, aerospace engineering, and furniture production.


South Korea, officially the Republic of Korea, occupies the southern half of the Korean Peninsula in East Asia, spanning roughly thirty-eight thousand square miles between the Yellow Sea to the west and the Sea of Japan, known locally as the East Sea, to the east. South Korea's physical geography is intensely mountainous, with steep hills and uplands covering nearly seventy percent of the nation's total land area. The rugged Taebaek Mountain Range forms the spine of the peninsula along the eastern coast, while the southern coast is heavily indented with thousands of rocky islands and inlets. Off the southern coast lies Jeju Island, a dramatic volcanic island dominated by Hallasan, a shield volcano and South Korea's highest peak at six thousand three hundred and ninety-eight feet. The fertile western and southern coastal plains, drained by the Han, Geum, and Nakdong Rivers, support the majority of the nation's population and agriculture. Culturally and industrially, South Korea has evolved into a global economic and technological powerhouse, centered in the Seoul Capital Area, which houses half the national population. Despite lacking significant domestic mineral resources, South Korea leads the world in semiconductor fabrication, display technology, commercial shipbuilding, automotive manufacturing, and consumer electronics.
