Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Alaska and the African nation of South Africa.
Alaska, admitted to the Union in 1959 as the forty-ninth state, is by far the largest state in the United States by geographic area. Encompassing more than six hundred and sixty-three thousand square miles, Alaska is more than twice the size of Texas and spans across four distinct physiographic regions: the Pacific Mountain System, the Interior, the Arctic Coastal Plain, and the rugged Brooks Mountain Range. Along its southern reaches, towering glaciated peaks dominate the landscape, culminating in Denali, the highest mountain peak in North America, rising twenty thousand three hundred and ten feet above sea level. Alaska boasts more coastline than all other forty-nine states combined, bordered by the Pacific Ocean, the Bering Sea, and the Arctic Ocean, while the Aleutian volcanic island arc stretches over a thousand miles westward toward Asia. Geographically isolated from the contiguous United States, Alaska features extraordinary ecological diversity, from temperate coastal rainforests in the southeastern panhandle to vast boreal forests and treeless Arctic tundra underlain by permafrost. Economically, Alaska relies heavily on its abundant natural resources, including petroleum extraction from the North Slope, prolific commercial maritime fisheries in the Bering Sea, and a vibrant wilderness tourism industry.
South Africa, officially the Republic of South Africa, occupies the southernmost tip of the African continent, bordered by Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Eswatini, and completely encircling the kingdom of Lesotho. Spanning roughly four hundred and seventy-one thousand square miles, South Africa features a dramatic physical geography characterized by an expansive interior plateau, known as the Highveld, rimmed by the rugged Great Escarpment, whose highest peaks rise in the Drakensberg mountains to over eleven thousand feet. The country is bordered by two oceans, with the cold Atlantic Ocean along the western coast and the warm Indian Ocean to the east, converging at Cape Agulhas, the southernmost point of Africa. South Africa is uniquely structured with three distinct constitutional capitals: Pretoria serves as the executive administrative center, Cape Town houses the national legislature, and Bloemfontein hosts the judicial seat. Famed for its extraordinary biodiversity, South Africa spans diverse ecological biomes ranging from the arid semi-desert of the Karoo to the subtropical eastern coast and the world-renowned floral kingdom of the Cape Peninsula, crowned by Table Mountain. Economically, South Africa is an industrial powerhouse with world-leading reserves of platinum, chromium, and gold.
