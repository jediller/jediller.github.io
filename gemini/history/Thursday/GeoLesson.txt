Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Minnesota and the North African kingdom of Morocco.


Minnesota, known as the Land of Ten Thousand Lakes, is located in the Upper Midwest region of the United States. Covering approximately eighty-seven thousand square miles, it ranks as the twelfth-largest state by land area. Minnesota is celebrated for its extraordinary glaciated landscape, which left behind more than eleven thousand freshwater lakes, pine forests, and rich agricultural prairies. In the northern wilderness lies the pristine Boundary Waters Canoe Area and the rocky North Shore of Lake Superior, the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area. In the north-central region, Lake Itasca serves as the legendary source of the mighty Mississippi River, which flows southward, carving scenic river bluffs. In the west, the flat, fertile Red River Valley supports grain agriculture. The Twin Cities of Minneapolis and Saint Paul straddle the Mississippi and Minnesota rivers, serving as the commercial and cultural hub. Economically, Minnesota combines agriculture in corn, soybeans, and dairy with a diversified economy renowned for medical technology, food processing, timber, and iron ore extraction from the Mesabi Range, shipped globally through the inland port of Duluth.


Morocco, officially the Kingdom of Morocco, is located in the northwestern corner of Africa, bordered by the Atlantic Ocean, the Mediterranean Sea, Algeria, and Mauritania. Spanning roughly one hundred and seventy-two thousand square miles, Morocco occupies a strategic position commanding the southern shore of the Strait of Gibraltar, where Africa and Europe lie only nine miles apart. The physical geography of Morocco is dominated by two mountain chains: the Rif Mountains along the Mediterranean coast and the towering Atlas Mountains, which culminate in Mount Toubkal at thirteen thousand six hundred and seventy feet. These mountains act as a climatic barrier, separating fertile Atlantic coastal plains from the arid expanses of the Sahara Desert to the south and east. Economically, Morocco leverages its geography as a global leader in phosphate mining, possessing roughly seventy percent of the world's known reserves. The country has also emerged as a green energy pioneer, home to the massive Noor Ouarzazate solar complex, while sustaining major agricultural exports, automotive manufacturing, and maritime logistics anchored by the deepwater port of Tanger-Med.
