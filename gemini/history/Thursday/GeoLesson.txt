Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Colorado and the North African kingdom of Morocco.
Colorado, celebrated as the Centennial State, is a landlocked Mountain West state spanning roughly one hundred and four thousand square miles, making it the eighth-largest state by area. Its rectangular borders enclose one of the highest and most dramatic landscapes in North America, divided cleanly between the flat, semi-arid High Plains in the east and the towering Southern Rocky Mountains across the central and western two-thirds of the state. Rising above fourteen thousand feet across more than fifty peaks, including Mount Elbert at fourteen thousand four hundred and forty feet, the Rockies form the Continental Divide, giving birth to major river systems including the Colorado, Rio Grande, Arkansas, and South Platte rivers. Further west, the mountains give way to the rugged mesas, canyons, and high plateaus of the Colorado Plateau. Colorado's semi-arid continental climate brings abundant sunshine alongside heavy winter mountain snowpack, which fuels a diverse economy anchored by aerospace and technology along the Front Range corridor from Denver to Colorado Springs, irrigated agriculture, energy production, and world-renowned alpine tourism.
Morocco, officially the Kingdom of Morocco, occupies the northwestern corner of Africa, uniquely positioned with coastlines along both the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Mediterranean Sea to the north, separated from Spain by the narrow Strait of Gibraltar. Covering roughly one hundred and seventy-two thousand square miles, Morocco's physical geography is dominated by the rugged Atlas Mountains, including the High Atlas range crowned by Jebel Toubkal at thirteen thousand six hundred and seventy-one feet, alongside the Rif Mountains bordering the Mediterranean. Between the mountains and the Atlantic lies a fertile, temperate coastal plain supporting major cities such as Casablanca and Rabat, while the southern and eastern slopes descend abruptly into the arid expanses of the Sahara Desert. Morocco's strategic maritime location at the gateway between the Atlantic, the Mediterranean, and Europe makes it a vital hub for international shipping, renewable solar energy, phosphate mining, automotive manufacturing, agriculture, and cultural tourism centered on Marrakech and Fez.
