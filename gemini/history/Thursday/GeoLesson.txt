Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Michigan and the East Asian nation of South Korea.
Michigan, known as the Great Lakes State, sits in the Upper Midwest and is uniquely split into two distinct landmasses, the Upper Peninsula and the Lower Peninsula, separated by the Straits of Mackinac and connected by the five-mile Mackinac Bridge. Covering roughly ninety-six thousand seven hundred square miles including extensive water territory, Michigan borders four of the five Great Lakes: Superior, Michigan, Huron, and Erie, giving it the longest freshwater coastline in the United States. The Lower Peninsula features fertile glacial plains, rolling hills, and major urban centers including Detroit, Grand Rapids, and Lansing, while the rugged Upper Peninsula contains dense northern forests, copper and iron ranges in the Porcupine Mountains, and dramatic sandstone cliffs along Lake Superior. Michigan's humid continental climate is moderated by lake-effect breezes that support thriving cherry, apple, and blueberry orchards alongside automotive manufacturing, mobility engineering, defense research, and Great Lakes maritime commerce.
South Korea, officially the Republic of Korea, occupies the southern half of the Korean Peninsula in East Asia, sharing a heavily fortified northern border with North Korea while facing the Yellow Sea to the west, the Korea Strait to the south, and the Sea of Japan to the east. Spanning roughly thirty-eight thousand seven hundred square miles, South Korea is predominantly mountainous, with the Taebaek Mountains running along the eastern spine and giving way to fertile alluvial plains and river basins in the west and south, where the capital Seoul and major port Busan are located. Nearly four thousand islands, led by volcanic Jeju Island off the southern coast, dot its deeply indented southern and western shores. Experiencing four distinct seasons shaped by East Asian monsoons, South Korea's coastal harbors and concentrated urban corridors power one of the world's most advanced industrial economies, anchored by semiconductor fabrication, shipbuilding, automobiles, consumer electronics, and international shipping.
