Today's United States location is Minnesota. Situated in the Upper Midwestern region of the United States, Minnesota is bounded by Canada to the north, Lake Superior and Wisconsin to the east, Iowa to the south, and South Dakota and North Dakota to the west. Known as the Land of 10,000 Lakes, Minnesota actually contains nearly twelve thousand lake basins carved by ancient glacial activity. Its physical geography transitions from the dense boreal forests and rocky Precambrian Canadian Shield in the northeast to the fertile agricultural prairies and rolling till plains in the south and west.


Minnesota's physical geography is dominated by major hydrological headwaters. The Mississippi River originates at Lake Itasca in northwestern Minnesota and flows south, forming a major national transportation artery. The state's northern Mesabi Iron Range supplies the vast majority of domestically mined iron ore in the United States. Fertile glacial soils support extensive corn, soybean, and sugar beet agriculture, while major urban centers such as Minneapolis and Saint Paul form a thriving economic, healthcare, and medical technology hub in the Upper Midwest.


Today's country is Brazil. Occupying nearly half of the South American continent, Brazil is the fifth largest nation in the world by both area and population. It borders every South American country except Chile and Ecuador, with an extensive four-thousand-mile coastline along the Atlantic Ocean. Brazil's physical geography is defined by two primary landforms: the immense Amazon River Basin in the north and northwest, featuring the world's largest tropical rainforest and river network, and the vast Brazilian Highlands in the south and central regions, characterized by elevated plateaus, rolling hills, and mountain ranges.


Brazil's immense physical terrain makes it a global agricultural and industrial powerhouse. The fertile plains of the central Cerrado plateau and southern regions produce massive global exports of soybeans, coffee, sugarcane, beef, and poultry. Rich subterranean mineral deposits supply iron ore, bauxite, and gold, while its extensive river systems provide hydroelectric power that supplies over sixty percent of Brazil's national electricity grid. Major urban and industrial centers include Sao Paulo, the financial heart of South America, and Rio de Janeiro along the southeastern Atlantic coast.
