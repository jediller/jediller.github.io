Officially nicknamed the Granite State, New Hampshire is situated in the northern portion of the New England region, bordered by Quebec, Canada to the north and northwest, Maine and the Atlantic Ocean to the east, Massachusetts to the south, and Vermont to the west along the Connecticut River. Covering approximately nine thousand three hundred forty-nine square miles, New Hampshire features three primary geographic regions: the Coastal Lowlands along its eighteen-mile Atlantic shoreline, the Eastern New England Upland characterized by rolling hills and pristine lakes like Lake Winnipesaukee, and the rugged White Mountains in the north, home to Mount Washington, the highest peak in the northeastern United States at six thousand two hundred eighty-eight feet.


New Hampshire's physical landscape is defined by dense northern hardwood and coniferous forests, swift rivers such as the Merrimack and Connecticut, and extensive granite formations. The state capital, Concord, is located in the Merrimack Valley, while Manchester and Nashua form the state's largest industrial, financial, and high-technology manufacturing centers. Historic Portsmouth on the Piscataqua River serves as a deepwater commercial port and naval shipyard hub. New Hampshire's economy is anchored by precision engineering, advanced electrical machinery, aerospace components, tourism centered on year-round mountain recreation, and sustainable forest products.
