Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Minnesota and the South American nation of Peru.


Minnesota, celebrated as the Land of 10,000 Lakes and the North Star State, is situated in the Upper Midwest region of the United States. Covering approximately eighty-seven thousand square miles as the twelfth-largest state, Minnesota borders Canada to the north, Lake Superior and Wisconsin to the east, Iowa to the south, and North and South Dakota to the west. Shaped profoundly by repeated Pleistocene glaciations, Minnesota boasts over eleven thousand lakes and thousands of freshwater wetlands, including the legendary headwaters of the Mississippi River at Lake Itasca in the north-central forests. The state features diverse physical regions: the rugged Canadian Shield and Sawtooth Mountains along the Lake Superior North Shore; the vast wilderness of the Boundary Waters Canoe Area; the mineral-rich Mesabi Iron Range; the central hardwood forests; and the fertile prairie plains and glacial Lake Agassiz basin of the Red River Valley in the west. Economically, Minnesota pairs agricultural grain and livestock production with iron ore mining, biomedical technology, financial services, and retail commerce centered in the Twin Cities of Minneapolis and Saint Paul, alongside the vital freshwater international shipping port of Duluth on Lake Superior.


Peru, officially the Republic of Peru, occupies an extraordinarily diverse geographic position along the western Pacific coast of South America, spanning roughly four hundred and ninety-six thousand square miles as the third-largest nation on the continent. It borders Ecuador and Colombia to the north, Brazil to the east, Bolivia to the southeast, and Chile to the south. Peru is classically divided into three distinct physiographic regions: the Costa, a narrow, hyper-arid coastal desert strip cooled by the northward-flowing Humboldt Current; the Sierra, the towering Andean mountain spine containing ice-capped peaks such as Huascaran at twenty-two thousand two hundred and five feet, high-altitude alpine plateaus, and Lake Titicaca, the world's highest navigable lake; and the Selva, a vast expanse of lush Amazonian rainforest covering over sixty percent of Peru's territory where the Maranon and Ucayali Rivers unite to form the Amazon River. Historically the heart of the Inca Empire, centered in Cusco and the cloud-forest citadel of Machu Picchu, Peru leverages its varied geography to drive an economy founded on copper, silver, and gold mining, rich Pacific commercial fisheries, tropical agriculture, expanding green energy, and a globally celebrated gastronomic capital in Lima.
