Utah, known as the Beehive State, is located in the Mountain West region of the United States. Covering nearly eighty-five thousand square miles, Utah ranks as the thirteenth-largest state by area. It borders Idaho to the north, Wyoming to the northeast, Colorado to the east, Arizona to the south, and Nevada to the west, meeting Colorado, New Mexico, and Arizona at the Four Corners. Utah's physical geography spans three distinct provinces: the Rocky Mountains, the Colorado Plateau, and the Basin and Range province. The Wasatch Range and the Uinta Mountains, crowned by Kings Peak at thirteen thousand five hundred and twenty-eight feet, form the state's mountainous spine. Along the western base of the Wasatch Range lies the Wasatch Front, where roughly eighty percent of Utah's population lives, anchored by Salt Lake City, Provo, and Ogden. To the west lies the arid Great Basin, home to the Great Salt Lake and the Bonneville Salt Flats. To the south and east, the Colorado Plateau showcases red rock canyons and sandstone arches across five iconic national parks: Zion, Bryce Canyon, Arches, Canyonlands, and Capitol Reef. Economically, Utah thrives on software engineering in the Silicon Slopes, aerospace, biomedical research, copper mining at Bingham Canyon, and outdoor tourism.


Norway, officially the Kingdom of Norway, occupies the western and northern portion of the Scandinavian Peninsula in Northern Europe, bordering Sweden, Finland, and Russia along the North Sea, the Norwegian Sea, and the Barents Sea. Covering approximately one hundred and forty-nine thousand square miles, Norway possesses a dramatic coastline deeply indented by thousands of coastal islands and glacier-carved fjords, including Geirangerfjord and Sognefjord. The Scandinavian Mountains form the country's rugged interior, crowned by Galdhopiggen at eight thousand one hundred feet, with territory extending to the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard. Despite its northern latitude, Norway enjoys a relatively mild maritime climate along its shores due to the North Atlantic Current. Norway has transformed its coastal geography into an exceptional economic powerhouse with one of the world's highest living standards. The nation leverages North Sea offshore energy reserves to export petroleum and natural gas, channeling revenues into its Government Pension Fund Global. Norway also excels in commercial maritime shipping, hydroelectric power supplying nearly all domestic electricity, offshore wind, and salmon aquaculture, with the capital of Oslo anchoring green finance and maritime innovation.
