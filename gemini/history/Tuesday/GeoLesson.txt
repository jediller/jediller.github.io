Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Minnesota and the South American nation of Peru.
Minnesota, known as the North Star State and the Land of Ten Thousand Lakes, lies in the Upper Midwest along the northern border of the United States, bounded by the Canadian provinces of Manitoba and Ontario to the north, Lake Superior and Wisconsin to the east, Iowa to the south, and North Dakota and South Dakota to the west. Covering roughly eighty-six thousand nine hundred square miles as the twelfth-largest state, Minnesota was sculpted by continental ice sheets that left behind nearly twelve thousand lakes, vast peatlands, and the headwaters of the Mississippi River at Lake Itasca. The state spans three distinct ecological biomes: rugged boreal pine forests and iron-rich Mesabi Range hills in the northeast, deciduous hardwood forests through the central lakes and the Twin Cities of Minneapolis and Saint Paul, and fertile tallgrass prairie plains across the south and west. Minnesota's continental climate brings warm summers and cold, snowy winters, supporting an economy anchored by medical technology, retail headquarters, taconite iron mining, precision manufacturing, and productive corn and soybean agriculture.
Peru, officially the Republic of Peru, sits along the central Pacific coast of western South America, bordered by Ecuador and Colombia to the north, Brazil to the east, Bolivia to the southeast, Chile to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Spanning roughly four hundred and ninety-six thousand square miles as the third-largest country in South America, Peru encompasses three dramatically contrasting geographic zones. Along the western edge stretches the Costa, an arid coastal desert plain cooled by the Humboldt Current and home to the capital city of Lima. Rising sharply inland is the Sierra, formed by the towering Andes Mountains with snow-capped peaks exceeding twenty thousand feet, deep canyons, and high-altitude Lake Titicaca along the Bolivian border. East of the Andes lies the Selva, a vast tropical Amazon Basin rainforest drained by the headwaters of the Amazon River. Peru's extraordinary topographic and climatic diversity underpins a resource-rich economy driven by copper, gold, and silver mining, Pacific anchovy fisheries, specialty agriculture, natural gas, and world-renowned cultural tourism centered on Cusco and Machu Picchu.
