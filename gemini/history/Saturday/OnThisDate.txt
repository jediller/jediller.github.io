In 1877, American inventor Thomas Edison publicly announced his creation of the phonograph, the first machine capable of both recording and playing back sound. Working at his Menlo Park laboratory in New Jersey, Edison had wrapped a sheet of tinfoil around a grooved metal cylinder and spoken the nursery rhyme Mary Had a Little Lamb into a mouthpiece connected to a vibrating needle. When he turned the crank and heard his own voice reproduced clearly, he amazed onlookers and launched the global recorded music and audio industry.

In 1964, the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge opened to vehicular traffic across New York Harbor, linking the boroughs of Brooklyn and Staten Island for the first time by road. Designed by master bridge engineer Othmar Ammann and named for the Italian explorer Giovanni da Verrazzano, its main double-deck suspension span of four thousand two hundred and sixty feet made it the longest suspension bridge in the world at the time of its completion, transforming regional commerce and commuter transportation across the greater metropolitan New York region.
