Welcome to today's geography lesson exploring the United States state of Oregon and the Scandinavian nation of Norway.


Oregon, celebrated as the Beaver State, is situated in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. Spanning roughly ninety-eight thousand square miles as the ninth-largest state by area, Oregon is defined by dramatic physiographic diversity sculpted by volcanic activity and tectonic plate collisions. The rugged Pacific coastline features towering sea stacks, dynamic sand dunes, and temperate rainforests. Moving inland, the fertile Willamette Valley contains over seventy percent of the state's population, housing the major cultural and economic centers of Portland, Salem, and Eugene. East of the valley rises the formidable Cascade Mountain Range, a volcanic chain crowned by Mount Hood at eleven thousand two hundred and forty-nine feet and Crater Lake, the deepest lake in the United States, formed within the collapsed caldera of ancient Mount Mazama. East of the Cascades, the terrain transitions into the semi-arid high desert of the Columbia Plateau and Great Basin, framed by the dramatic Columbia River Gorge to the north and the deeply carved Hells Canyon to the east along the Idaho border. Economically, Oregon pairs rich timber harvesting, hazelnut orchards, grass seed cultivation, and pinot noir viticulture with world-class athletic sportswear design, clean hydroelectric power, and semiconductor manufacturing within the Silicon Forest.


Norway, officially the Kingdom of Norway, occupies the western and northern portion of the Scandinavian Peninsula in Northern Europe, bordered by Sweden, Finland, and Russia, with extensive coastlines along the North Sea, the Norwegian Sea, and the Arctic Barents Sea. Encompassing approximately one hundred and forty-nine thousand square miles, including the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard, Norway is celebrated for its extraordinary maritime geography and glaciated terrain. Thousands of deeply incised fjords, including the iconic Geirangerfjord and Sognefjord, carve into the rugged Scandinavian Mountains, which span the nation's spine like an alpine backbone. The warm waters of the Norwegian Current keep coastal ports ice-free year-round despite the high northern latitude. Highly mountainous and forested, Norway leverages its abundant alpine precipitation to generate nearly all of its domestic electricity from clean hydropower. Economically and strategically, Norway is a global maritime and energy titan, prudently investing petroleum and natural gas revenues from the North Sea into the world's largest sovereign wealth fund, while leading the world in commercial shipping, salmon aquaculture, offshore marine engineering, and electric vehicle adoption.
