Today is Monday, August seventeenth, two thousand twenty-six. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last twenty-four hours.

President Donald Trump said in a Sunday social media post that he had instructed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to substantially reduce the annual United States military exercises with South Korea. Trump called the exercises costly and an inappropriate and hostile signal to North Korea, cited his very good relationship with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, and complained that South Korea had declined to join the United States war against Iran. Those were Trump's stated reasons for the order. South Korea's Defense Ministry said the training began Monday as planned.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said on Meet the Press that he did not support the Democratic Socialists of America agenda as the organization had articulated it. He specifically rejected defunding the police, open borders, and abolishing the Senate. Asked about Medicare for All, Jeffries said it was not legislation he currently supported, while adding that the country must fix its broken health care system and make care affordable for every American.

Senator Bernie Sanders used a public post to criticize Google co-founder Sergey Brin for spending more than one hundred million dollars against California's proposed one-time five percent tax on billionaire wealth. Sanders argued that Brin could pay roughly fourteen billion dollars under the proposal and remain worth about two hundred seventy billion dollars. His post presented the spending as an example of billionaire political power.

Attorney General Todd Blanche said on Fox News Sunday that President Trump should have a voice in decisions about individual federal prosecutions. Blanche declined to promise Justice Department independence from the White House, but said Trump would expect him to say no when they disagreed about the law. The comments described Blanche's view of presidential influence over prosecutorial decisions.

Democratic Senator Mark Kelly criticized Trump's order reducing the South Korea exercises, calling it shortsighted and a mistake. Kelly said the joint training has been vital to deterring North Korea and preparing American and South Korean forces to respond together.
