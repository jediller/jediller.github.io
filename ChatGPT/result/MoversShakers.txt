Today is Thursday, August 6, 2026. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last 24 hours.

President Donald Trump used a speech at a Las Vegas casino to promote his economic record and urge Republicans to vote in November. He promised that a Republican Congress would pass what he called the Great Healthcare Plan, centered on money for individual health savings accounts. He also claimed American exports had reached the highest level in history. Government data released Tuesday instead showed that June exports fell from May, although exports for the year to date were higher. Discussing the Iran war, Trump again predicted that oil prices would fall when the conflict ends, then added that the United States might have to send prices higher, without explaining what he meant. He said a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could be announced this week. Trump also called Canada nasty while saying he loved Canadians, praised development of Nevada’s Thacker Pass lithium mine, and said he would like to fire the Supreme Court’s three liberal justices before linking that remark to Democratic proposals to expand the court.

Trump sharply attacked Abdul El-Sayed, the newly declared Democratic Senate nominee in Michigan, calling him a communist and accusing him of hating Jewish people. El-Sayed rejected that characterization and said his commitment to Jewish safety was as strong as his commitment to the safety of his own children. He called for ending wars and reducing corporate influence in politics.

Senator Bernie Sanders told reporters that El-Sayed’s narrow primary victory was an extraordinary defeat of the Democratic establishment and a sign of change he considered unstoppable. Sanders argued that the nominee’s economic program could appeal across Democratic constituencies and to some working-class Republicans. Responding to questions about El-Sayed’s criticism of Israel, Sanders said criticism of Benjamin Netanyahu’s government and its policies in Gaza is not antisemitic.

Senate Majority Leader Charles Schumer, who had backed El-Sayed’s opponent, said after speaking with the nominee that El-Sayed was a very good candidate with strengths that could bring new voters into the party. Schumer said they would work together to win Michigan and take back the Senate.

Vice President J. D. Vance said in a Fox News interview that aired Wednesday that Trump telephoned him when his newest son was born and suggested he take time off. Vance joked that the offer amounted to one day because they had a meeting scheduled the next morning.

North Carolina Representative Chuck Edwards announced that he was ending his reelection campaign after the House Ethics Committee recommended censure over his conduct toward two female aides. Edwards said the decision followed prayer and reflection, thanked western North Carolina, and said he would complete his term. He has denied intending any sexual or romantic advance.
