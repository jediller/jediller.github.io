Today is Friday, August 7, 2026. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last 24 hours.

President Donald Trump signed two immigration actions Thursday and said one would narrow eligibility for citizenship among people born in the United States, while the other would restrict visas used for what he called birth tourism. Trump said he believed the actions were constitutional. The Supreme Court had rejected his earlier attempt in June and upheld a broad reading of birthright citizenship.

Asked about United States weapons supplies, Trump said the country needs more munitions all the time. He claimed some types were available in virtually unlimited supply but acknowledged that others were a little bit tighter, without naming them. He separately defended Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, said he was extremely happy with Hegseth’s work, and called reporting about friction between them treasonous. Discussing the Strait of Hormuz, Trump described it as sort of open and declined to say whether a deal with Iran had been reached.

Trump also declined to say whether Ohio Representative Max Miller should leave Congress following domestic abuse allegations made by Miller’s former wife, calling the situation very sad and saying he would not get involved. When asked about reports that he had privately encouraged donors to support Vice President J. D. Vance for president in two thousand twenty-eight, Trump called Vance great but said it was far too early for an endorsement.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told Axios that the administration intended to keep closing what he called escape valves around Cuba’s government. He said Cuban leaders could not simply wait out sanctions and argued that patience and persistence had been lost in American foreign policy. Rubio predicted that by the end of the administration Cuba would at least be on an irreversible path toward a different future. The administration imposed additional sanctions Thursday. Rubio’s comments described his own pressure campaign; they were not a statement by Cuban officials.

Senator Rand Paul said Anthony Fauci’s presidential pardon meant the former federal health official could not rely on fear of prosecution to avoid answering Senate questions about the pandemic response. Paul’s committee voted Thursday to hold Fauci in contempt and refer the matter to the Justice Department. Fauci had invoked his Fifth Amendment right more than one hundred times at a hearing the previous week.
