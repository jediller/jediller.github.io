Today is Tuesday, August eleventh, two thousand twenty-six. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last twenty-four hours.

President Donald Trump called his new childhood-vaccine executive order a major victory for parents’ rights, religious and constitutional rights, and what he described as gold-standard science. He urged separate appointments for childhood immunizations and separate measles, mumps, and rubella shots. Trump again suggested that the number or timing of vaccines could contribute to autism, although decades of research have found no causal link. His order advises states to reconsider school-vaccine schedules, but states retain authority over those requirements.

In a separate social-media statement about Iran, Trump rejected Tehran’s demand for compensation for wartime damage. He said he had instructed American negotiators to demand compensation from Iran for people killed or wounded by Iran-backed forces and for families of Iranian protesters. The statement hardened the public negotiating posture as Iran continued to condition reopening the Strait of Hormuz on sanctions relief, release of frozen assets, an end to the American blockade, and compensation.

Republican Senator Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, a physician and chairman of the Senate health committee, issued a direct rebuke of the vaccine order. Cassidy said vaccines are overwhelmingly safe and effective, do not cause autism, and that the president lacked the expertise to make the proposed changes. He warned that requiring more separate visits could increase hesitancy and leave children less protected, and urged parents to rely on their pediatricians.
