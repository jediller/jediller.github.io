Today is Tuesday, August 4, 2026. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last 24 hours.

President Donald Trump said newly planned contacts with Tehran were Iran's last chance to reach a deal and avoid an escalation of United States strikes. He described a first phase focused on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and a later phase addressing Iran's nuclear program, while Iran publicly denied that it was negotiating with Washington and said its talks were with Oman. Trump responded that the United States and Iran were in fact talking, whether Iran admitted it or not. In separate remarks, Trump said Chevron and Exxon Mobil had made too much money from elevated oil prices and should give some of it back to the public by lowering retail prices. He also defended a settlement protecting him, his sons, and the Trump Organization from some past Internal Revenue Service audits, saying the government had been weaponized against him. He claimed, without supplying evidence, that independent surveys showing his approval ratings falling were false.

Senate Democratic leader Charles Schumer criticized the administration's decision to end a temporary Medicare Part D subsidy after this year. He said the administration was choosing to raise prescription-drug costs for twenty-five million seniors and called the decision heartless and cruel. Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Mehmet Oz defended the change, saying it would keep billions of taxpayer dollars from being funneled to insurance companies. He said most beneficiaries would see monthly increases of less than ten dollars and that some premiums could fall.

New York Attorney General Letitia James, announcing a lawsuit joined by twenty-four other states, said the administration was again trying to raise taxes on families and businesses after losing a tariff case at the Supreme Court. The suit argues that new double-digit tariffs on fifty-nine countries and the European Union unlawfully replace import duties the court struck down in February.

Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley said he had invited former special counsel Jack Smith to testify in September because the public deserved full transparency and accountability concerning records obtained during investigations of Trump. Lawyers for Smith say he testified truthfully and did nothing wrong.

Elon Musk was not personally quoted in the newly filed challenge to Minnesota's artificial-intelligence law. His company xAI argued in court that the state's ban on so-called nudification technology reaches beyond nonconsensual fake nude images, covers protected expression, and lacks a safe harbor for good-faith prevention efforts. Minnesota says the first-in-the-nation law is intended to stop technology used to create intimate images of real people without consent.
