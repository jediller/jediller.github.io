Today is Thursday, August 20, 2026. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last 24 hours.

President Donald Trump said the United States would launch what he called an economic D-Day against Iran and threatened major economic consequences for any country that helps or does business with Tehran. His announcement described an escalation of economic pressure, but it did not spell out every enforcement step.

Elon Musk wrote on X that former Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin had been unjustly convicted of murdering George Floyd and should be freed. Musk offered the conclusion as his view. Chauvin's convictions remain in force, and his newly filed petition seeking to overturn the state murder conviction has not been decided.

Senator Bernie Sanders joined workers in Saint Albans, Vermont, who are trying to prevent Dairy Farmers of America from closing a milk-processing plant and adjoining creamery. Sanders urged the company to keep the facilities open and bargain with workers, presenting the dispute as a test of whether a large agricultural cooperative will protect rural jobs and local dairy infrastructure.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani allowed a City Council bill promising a ten-thousand-dollar boost for school paraprofessionals to become law without his signature, while his administration moved to block it in court. Mamdani argued that compensation must be negotiated through collective bargaining and that the council had improperly tried to replace that process through legislation.

California Governor Gavin Newsom used a back-to-school event to promote the statewide rollout of universal transitional kindergarten and two free meals, breakfast and lunch, for every public-school student. He framed the programs, together with early-literacy, community-school, and after-school investments, as permanent support for children and families rather than temporary relief.

Florida Governor Ron DeSantis announced sixty million dollars in Florida Cancer Innovation Fund awards and opened a new seventy-million-dollar application cycle. He said the state is backing research and treatment projects that can produce practical improvements for cancer patients and emphasized Florida's cumulative investment in cancer research during his administration.

Bill Ackman and Neri Oxman announced that they are giving ten million Pershing Square shares, valued in current reporting at roughly four hundred million dollars, to support a new brain-research and care institute. Ackman connected the project to his family's experience after his daughter survived a catastrophic brain hemorrhage and said the goal is faster scientific progress and better care for severe brain injuries.
