Today is Wednesday, August nineteenth, two thousand twenty-six. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last twenty-four hours.

President Donald Trump celebrated his preferred candidates' Tuesday primary victories on social media, writing that there had been many great wins. His endorsed candidate Byron Donalds won Florida's Republican nomination for governor, and Senator Ashley Moody won the Republican nomination in Florida's special United States Senate race.

Byron Donalds, after winning Florida's Republican nomination for governor, promised to apply a state version of the Department of Government Efficiency to every local government and state agency, saying there would be no sacred cows. He also promised to publish state spending online. Donalds thanked Trump for his endorsement but said it would not carry him through the general election by itself.

Michigan Senator Elissa Slotkin asked Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman Dan Caine to state that federal troops would not be sent to polling places or used to seize election materials in November. Slotkin said she believed Trump was laying groundwork to challenge midterm results. The Pentagon said it would respond directly to her letter; the Joint Chiefs had not publicly responded.

Federal Communications Commissioner Anna Gomez praised Disney and ABC for suing to block an FCC broadcast-license proceeding. Gomez said the agency had used license threats to punish speech the administration disliked and argued that other broadcasters should take the lawsuit as encouragement to resist government pressure.

Representative Chellie Pingree said a man killed by an Immigration and Customs Enforcement agent in Maine was not the intended target of the operation and was not subject to a final removal order. She called that disclosure a shocking contradiction of earlier federal accounts and said basic questions about the shooting remained unanswered. The Department of Homeland Security said it stood by its previous assessment.
