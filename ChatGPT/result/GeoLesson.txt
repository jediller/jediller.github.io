Today's state is Virginia.

Virginia lies in the Mid-Atlantic, between the Atlantic Ocean and the Appalachian Mountains. It borders Maryland and Washington, D.C., across the Potomac River to the north and northeast, North Carolina and Tennessee to the south, and Kentucky and West Virginia to the west. From east to west, the land crosses five broad regions: the low Coastal Plain, or Tidewater; the rolling Piedmont; the Blue Ridge; the long valleys and ridges of the Appalachians; and a small section of the Appalachian Plateau. Mount Rogers, in the southwest, is the state's highest point at five thousand seven hundred twenty-nine feet. The Potomac, Rappahannock, York, and James rivers flow toward Chesapeake Bay, whose sheltered waters encouraged early settlement, farming, fishing, and trade. Summers are generally warm and humid, while elevation makes the mountains cooler and snowier. Today, deep harbors around Hampton Roads support shipping and naval activity. Northern Virginia's position beside the nation's capital supports government and technology work, while the Shenandoah Valley and Piedmont remain important for farming. Much of the population follows an urban arc from Northern Virginia through Richmond to Hampton Roads.

Today's country is South Africa.

South Africa occupies the southern end of the African continent, with the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Indian Ocean to the east. It borders Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and Eswatini, and it completely surrounds the mountain kingdom of Lesotho. A high interior plateau dominates the country. The Great Escarpment drops toward narrower coastal lowlands, and the Drakensberg forms its loftiest section along Lesotho. The Orange River crosses the dry interior to the Atlantic, while the Vaal is its major tributary; the Limpopo marks part of the northern boundary. Climate varies sharply: the west is arid, the southwest around Cape Town has dry summers and winter rain, the east coast is humid and subtropical, and the elevated Highveld has warm summers and cool, dry winters. Johannesburg grew on the mineral-rich Witwatersrand and became the largest economic center. Pretoria is nearby on the plateau, while Durban, Cape Town, and Gqeberha developed as major ports. Geography shapes farming too: vineyards and wheat thrive in the southwest, maize on the Highveld, and sugarcane along the warm eastern coast. Water scarcity and uneven rainfall remain important limits on settlement and agriculture.
