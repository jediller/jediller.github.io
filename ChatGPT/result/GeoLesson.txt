Today's state is Utah.

Utah lies in the Mountain West and meets Arizona, Nevada, Idaho, Wyoming, Colorado, and New Mexico, with the last three helping form the Four Corners. Three major physical regions meet here. The Wasatch and Uinta ranges are part of the Rocky Mountain system. The western valleys and fault-block ranges belong to the Great Basin. Southern and eastern Utah rise into the Colorado Plateau, where rivers have cut canyons, arches, and mesas into colorful sedimentary rock. Kings Peak in the Uintas is the state's highest point at thirteen thousand five hundred twenty-eight feet.

The Great Salt Lake is a shallow terminal lake with no outlet. The Bear, Weber, and Jordan Rivers bring water and minerals into it, while evaporation removes water and leaves salts behind. Most Utahns live along the Wasatch Front, where mountain snowpack supplies water and a narrow corridor links Salt Lake City with other urban centers. The dry climate makes irrigation important, while snow, red-rock scenery, copper deposits, and public lands support winter sports, tourism, mining, and growing technology and service industries.

Today's country is Paraguay.

Paraguay is a landlocked country near the center of South America. It borders Bolivia to the northwest and north, Brazil to the northeast and east, and Argentina to the south and west. The Paraguay River runs from north to south and divides the country into two contrasting regions. East of the river, the Paraná Plateau and rolling lowlands receive more rain and hold most farms, cities, and people. West of it lies the Gran Chaco, a broad, hotter plain of scrub, savanna, wetlands, and seasonally flooded ground where settlements are sparse and distances are large.

The Paraguay and Paraná Rivers connect this inland country to ports farther downstream and help generate enormous amounts of hydroelectric power, especially at Itaipú on the Paraná. Asunción, the capital, stands on the Paraguay River in the more densely settled east. Summers are generally hot and rainy, while winters are cooler and drier, with greater heat and dryness toward the western Chaco. Fertile eastern soils, warm conditions, and river transport favor soybeans, corn, and other crops. Open grasslands support cattle, and abundant electricity has become a major national resource.
