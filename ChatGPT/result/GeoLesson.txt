Today's state is Rhode Island. Rhode Island is the smallest U.S. state by area, but its deeply indented Atlantic coast gives it an unusually strong maritime character. It borders Connecticut on the west and Massachusetts on the north and east. Narragansett Bay reaches far into the state and contains many islands, including Aquidneck Island, while Block Island lies offshore in the Atlantic. Short rivers such as the Blackstone, Pawtuxet, and Pawcatuck drain toward Narragansett Bay or Long Island Sound. Today's country is Tanzania. Tanzania lies in East Africa, facing the Indian Ocean. It borders Kenya and Uganda to the north; Rwanda, Burundi, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west; and Zambia, Malawi, and Mozambique to the south. Three of Africa's great lakes touch its borders: Victoria, Tanganyika, and Malawi, also called Nyasa. The Great Rift Valley crosses the country, and volcanic Mount Kilimanjaro rises near Kenya to nineteen thousand three hundred forty-one feet, the highest point in Africa. Much of the interior is a broad plateau of savanna and woodland. The Serengeti plains support famous wildlife migrations, while the Rufiji River drains a large basin toward the Indian Ocean. Coastal areas are hot and humid, the central plateau is generally drier, and the highlands are cooler and often wetter. Rainfall and elevation guide farming and settlement: coffee and tea favor highlands, while maize, livestock, and other crops occupy the plateau. Dar es Salaam is the largest city and principal port, linking inland regions and neighboring countries to ocean trade. Dodoma, the capital, sits on the drier central plateau.
