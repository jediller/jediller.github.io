Missouri.

Missouri lies near the center of the United States and touches eight states. Iowa is to the north; Illinois, Kentucky, and Tennessee are across the Mississippi River to the east; Arkansas is to the south; and Oklahoma, Kansas, and Nebraska are to the west. The Missouri River enters from the northwest, passes Kansas City, crosses the state, and joins the Mississippi just north of Saint Louis. These rivers made Missouri a gateway for trade, migration, and settlement.

Northern Missouri has rolling glacial plains and rich farmland. South of the Missouri River, the Ozark Plateau contains forested hills, springs, caves, and deeply cut streams. The southeastern Bootheel belongs to the flat, fertile Mississippi Alluvial Plain and is vulnerable to flooding and earthquakes from the New Madrid seismic zone. Missouri has a humid continental climate, with hot summers, cold winters, thunderstorms, tornadoes, droughts, and floods. Corn, soybeans, cattle, poultry, and timber are important, while the Ozarks contain major lead deposits. Kansas City and Saint Louis grew where rivers, railroads, highways, and industry meet.

Sudan.

Sudan lies in northeastern Africa. Egypt borders it on the north; the Red Sea, Eritrea, and Ethiopia are to the east; South Sudan is to the south; and the Central African Republic, Chad, and Libya are to the west. At Khartoum, the White Nile from central Africa meets the Blue Nile from the Ethiopian highlands. The combined Nile flows north through a narrow settled corridor toward Egypt. Seasonal rivers and wadis carry water only part of the year.

Much of northern Sudan is Sahara Desert, including the Nubian Desert, while the center grades through the semiarid Sahel into wetter savanna toward the south. Rainfall is highly seasonal and decreases sharply from south to north. The Red Sea Hills rise in the east, and volcanic Jebel Marra in Darfur contains the country's highest terrain. Heat, drought, desertification, and irregular rain make water access a central challenge. Most farming and cities cluster near the Nile or in better-watered central and southern zones. Irrigation supports crops such as wheat and cotton, while rain-fed farms grow sorghum, millet, sesame, and groundnuts. Pastoralists move cattle, sheep, goats, and camels with seasonal grazing. Port Sudan is the main seaport and links the interior to Red Sea trade.
