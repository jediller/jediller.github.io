Today’s state is Alaska.

Alaska occupies the far northwest of North America. Canada lies to the east, the Arctic Ocean to the north, and the Pacific Ocean to the south. The Bering Strait separates Alaska from Russia by about fifty miles at its narrowest point. The state includes the long Alaska Peninsula, the Aleutian Islands, and thousands of coastal islands. Mountain systems include the Alaska Range, where Denali rises as the highest peak in North America, the Brooks Range in the north, and coastal ranges shaped by glaciers and active volcanoes. Major rivers include the Yukon, Kuskokwim, and Copper.

Alaska spans Arctic, subarctic, and cool maritime climates. Permafrost and tundra dominate much of the north and west, while the southeast has temperate rain forest. Most settlements are isolated by mountains, water, or vast roadless distances, so aircraft and boats are essential. Anchorage grew beside a sheltered inlet and transportation corridor. Fishing supports coastal communities, oil production is concentrated on the North Slope, and mining, tourism, and federal activity are also important. Earthquakes, volcanoes, coastal storms, wildfire, melting permafrost, and retreating sea ice strongly affect infrastructure and daily life.

Today’s country is Uganda.

Uganda is a landlocked country in East Africa. It borders South Sudan, Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Much of the country lies on a broad plateau between the eastern and western branches of the East African Rift. Lake Victoria fills part of the southern border and is Africa’s largest lake by area. The White Nile leaves the lake at Jinja, crosses Lake Kyoga, and continues north toward South Sudan. The Rwenzori Mountains rise along the western border, while volcanic highlands and lakes mark the rift valleys.

Uganda lies near the equator, but elevation keeps much of the climate warm rather than extremely hot. Rainfall supports savanna, woodland, wetlands, and productive farmland, although the northeast is much drier. Kampala and nearby cities grew near Lake Victoria, where water, fertile land, and transport routes favor dense settlement. Farming employs many people and produces coffee, bananas, maize, tea, and livestock. Lake fisheries and hydropower on the Nile are important, while oil development near Lake Albert is expanding. Floods, landslides, drought, soil erosion, and pressure on forests and wetlands create continuing challenges.
