Today's state is Florida.

Florida occupies a long, low peninsula between the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico, with the Florida Straits separating its southern tip from Cuba. Georgia and Alabama form its northern land borders. The state has more coastline than any other in the contiguous United States. Much of its bedrock is porous limestone, so rainwater creates springs, caves, and sinkholes. Lake Okeechobee is the largest lake entirely within the state, and the slow-moving Everglades form a broad wetland south of it. The north-flowing Saint Johns River is an unusual major river because its gentle slope carries water toward the Atlantic.

Most of Florida is less than one hundred feet above sea level, and its highest natural point, Britton Hill, reaches only three hundred forty-five feet. The climate ranges from humid subtropical in the north to tropical near the Keys. Warm winters, beaches, and wetlands support tourism and citrus and vegetable farming, while ports and coastal cities link Florida to the Caribbean and Latin America. The same flat terrain and long coastline expose communities to hurricanes, storm surge, flooding, erosion, and saltwater intrusion. Water management therefore shapes farming, urban growth, and restoration of the Everglades.

Today's country is North Macedonia.

North Macedonia is a landlocked country in the central Balkan Peninsula. It borders Kosovo and Serbia to the north, Bulgaria to the east, Greece to the south, and Albania to the west. Mountains and basins dominate the landscape. Mount Korab, on the Albanian border, is the highest point at about nine thousand sixty-eight feet. The Vardar River drains much of the country southward into Greece and the Aegean Sea. Lakes Ohrid and Prespa lie along the Albanian frontier, while Lake Dojran sits near Greece. Ancient Lake Ohrid is especially deep and holds many species found nowhere else.

The Vardar valley is the country's main corridor for roads, railways, farms, and settlement. Skopje, the capital and largest city, stands beside the river in a broad basin. Summers are generally hot and dry in lower valleys, while winters are colder and snowier in the mountains. Rain shadows make some interior areas relatively dry. Irrigated valleys support grapes, tobacco, peppers, grains, and fruit, and mountain pastures support livestock. The country's position on a north-south Balkan route encourages trade, while lakes, gorges, ski areas, and historic towns support tourism. Mountain reservoirs also provide water and hydroelectric power.
