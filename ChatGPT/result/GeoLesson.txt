Today's state is Vermont.

Vermont lies in northern New England between New York and New Hampshire, with Massachusetts to the south and Quebec, Canada, to the north. The Green Mountains run from north to south through the state and give Vermont its name and rugged center. Mount Mansfield, at 4,393 feet, is the highest point. West of the mountains, the broad Champlain Valley slopes toward Lake Champlain and contains some of Vermont's most fertile farmland. The Connecticut River follows most of the eastern boundary and drains the eastern slopes. Forests cover much of the state, supporting wildlife, recreation, maple syrup production, and a strong fall tourism season. Mountain snow feeds streams, ski areas, and reservoirs, while narrow valleys concentrate roads, towns, and farms.

Today's country is Belarus.

Belarus is a landlocked country in Eastern Europe. It borders Russia, Ukraine, Poland, Lithuania, and Latvia. Its terrain is mostly flat to gently rolling because ice sheets shaped the landscape, leaving thousands of lakes and broad wetlands. Lake Narach is the largest lake. The Dnieper, Neman, and Pripyat are major rivers; their waters ultimately connect the country to the Black Sea or Baltic Sea basins. Southern Belarus contains part of Polesia, a vast region of marshes, peatlands, and floodplain forests. Large forests also cover the country, including the ancient Białowieża Forest along the Polish border. With no seacoast and few natural barriers, Belarus has long depended on overland routes and neighboring countries for trade. Its plains favor farming, especially grains, potatoes, and flax, while deposits of potash support fertilizer production.
