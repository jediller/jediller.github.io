Connecticut is in southern New England. Long Island Sound forms its saltwater coast, New York lies to the west, Massachusetts to the north, and Rhode Island to the east. The Connecticut River crosses the state from north to south through a broad central valley and reaches the Sound near Old Saybrook. Western Connecticut is part of the wooded Appalachian highlands, where the Taconic Mountains include the state's highest ground. The east is a landscape of low, rocky uplands cut by rivers such as the Thames, Housatonic, and Quinebaug. Connecticut has humid summers, cold winters, and four distinct seasons. Its sheltered coast and navigable rivers encouraged early ports and manufacturing towns. Today, the Interstate Ninety-One corridor follows the central valley through Hartford and New Haven, while the southwest is densely settled and closely linked to New York City. Today's country is Nepal.

Nepal is a landlocked South Asian country between India and China. Its terrain rises dramatically from the warm, low Terai plain along the Indian border, through steep middle hills and valleys, to the Himalayas. Eight of the world's fourteen mountains above eight thousand meters stand wholly or partly in Nepal, including Mount Everest on the border with China. Snow and glaciers feed swift rivers such as the Koshi, Gandaki, and Karnali, which flow south into the Ganges system. The summer monsoon supplies most rainfall, but totals vary sharply with elevation and mountain rain shadows. The fertile Terai supports farming and many fast-growing towns. Kathmandu and other historic cities occupy mountain basins where gentler land allows dense settlement. High valleys support herding, trade, and tourism but remain difficult to reach. Hydropower potential is immense, while earthquakes, landslides, floods, and glacial hazards make resilient roads and buildings essential.
