Today’s state is South Carolina.

South Carolina lies on the Atlantic coast of the southeastern United States. North Carolina borders it on the north, while Georgia lies across the Savannah River to the southwest. The land rises from sea level through three broad regions. The Coastal Plain covers about two-thirds of the state and includes beaches, tidal marshes, barrier and sea islands, the Santee River delta, and inland pine country. Near the Fall Line, rivers leave the harder Piedmont rocks and enter the flatter plain. The rolling Piedmont occupies much of the northwest, and a small Blue Ridge section reaches the Appalachian Mountains. Sassafras Mountain, on the North Carolina border, is the state’s highest point at about three thousand five hundred fifty feet.

Major rivers, including the Savannah, Santee, Pee Dee, and Edisto, generally flow southeast toward the Atlantic. Their water supports reservoirs, farms, wildlife habitat, and cities. Columbia, the capital, developed near the meeting of the Broad and Saluda rivers, which form the Congaree. Charleston grew around a deep coastal harbor and became a major port. A humid subtropical climate brings hot summers, mild winters, and ample rain. The coast supports tourism and shipping but is exposed to hurricanes, storm surge, flooding, and erosion. Fertile lowlands once encouraged rice and indigo agriculture, while the Piedmont later attracted textile mills and modern manufacturing.

Maldives is a long, narrow island country in the Indian Ocean, southwest of India and Sri Lanka. Its territory consists of about one thousand two hundred coral islands grouped into twenty-six natural atolls. The atolls sit on the crest of a submerged mountain ridge and are enclosed by reefs broken by channels, with shallow lagoons inside. Coral sand forms the dry land, and no island has a river or mountain. Average ground level is only about five feet above the sea, while the highest natural point is roughly eight feet. This makes Maldives the world’s lowest-lying country and leaves it unusually vulnerable to sea-level rise, coastal erosion, saltwater intrusion, storm waves, and flooding.

The tropical monsoon climate stays warm and humid throughout the year. Seasonal winds shift rainfall patterns, while surrounding ocean water moderates temperature. Reefs shelter islands and support rich marine life, but they are sensitive to warming and coral bleaching. Settlement is scattered because usable land is scarce. Malé, the crowded capital, is the main center of government, trade, and transportation, while boats and aircraft connect distant atolls. Fishing has long supported island communities, and tourism now depends on beaches, clear lagoons, reefs, and resort islands. Fresh water is limited, so communities rely on rainwater, groundwater lenses, and desalination. Geography therefore shapes nearly every part of Maldivian life, from travel and housing to food, jobs, and climate adaptation.
