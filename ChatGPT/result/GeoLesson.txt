United States State: Colorado

Colorado lies in the interior western United States and meets seven states. Its southwest corner touches Arizona at Four Corners. The Rocky Mountains cross the center and contain Mount Elbert, the state's highest point, while the Continental Divide separates water flowing toward the Pacific from water flowing toward the Atlantic and Gulf of Mexico. Headwaters of the Colorado, Rio Grande, Arkansas, and South Platte rivers begin in or near the high country. West of the Rockies, the Colorado Plateau and canyon country are drier. East of them, the land descends across the Piedmont into the Great Plains. Elevation shapes a varied climate: mountain areas are colder and snowier, while plains and western valleys are sunnier and more arid. Denver and other Front Range cities grew where plains transportation routes meet mountain resources and recreation. Snowpack supplies farms and cities far beyond the state, but drought and competing water demands are persistent concerns. Agriculture favors irrigated valleys and plains, while mining, energy, tourism, aerospace, and technology reflect Colorado's terrain and settlement pattern.

Sovereign Country: Palau

Palau is a western Pacific island country in Micronesia, east of the Philippines and north of Indonesia. Hundreds of islands form a long archipelago, but most people live on volcanic Babeldaob and nearby Koror. Babeldaob has forested hills, rivers, mangroves, and Palau's largest lake. Farther south, the Rock Islands are uplifted limestone formations surrounded by lagoons, coral reefs, and famous marine lakes. Palau has a tropical maritime climate with warm temperatures, high humidity, and heavy rain through much of the year. Its position near the equator places it outside the main typhoon belt, although storms, sea-level rise, coastal erosion, and coral bleaching remain hazards. Reefs and sheltered lagoons support extraordinary biodiversity, fishing, and tourism, while limited land and freshwater constrain farming and development. Koror became the commercial center because of its harbor and access to the islands, while the national capital, Ngerulmud, sits inland on Babeldaob. Conservation areas and visitor fees help protect the marine environment on which livelihoods depend.
