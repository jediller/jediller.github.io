New Mexico occupies the southwestern United States. Arizona lies to the west, Colorado to the north, Oklahoma and Texas to the east, and Mexico to the south. At the Four Corners, New Mexico also meets Utah at a single point. The Rio Grande flows south through the center of the state, linking mountain valleys and desert basins. The Continental Divide crosses western New Mexico. In the north, the Sangre de Cristo Mountains form the southern end of the Rocky Mountains and rise to Wheeler Peak, the state's highest point. The northwest belongs to the Colorado Plateau, the east opens onto the Great Plains, and the southwest contains rugged Basin and Range country.

Much of New Mexico is arid or semiarid, but elevation creates sharp differences. High mountains receive snow and support forests, while lower basins have hot summers and sparse grassland or desert vegetation. Albuquerque, Santa Fe, and many older communities grew along the Rio Grande, where water and flatter land favored settlement. Irrigation supports crops such as chile peppers, pecans, onions, and hay. Oil and gas production is important in the southeast and northwest, while copper, potash, renewable energy, military facilities, national laboratories, and tourism also shape the economy. Limited water, drought, wildfire, flash flooding, and erosion make careful management of rivers, forests, and aquifers essential.

Today’s country is Peru.

Peru lies on the Pacific coast of western South America. It borders Ecuador and Colombia to the north, Brazil to the east, Bolivia to the southeast, and Chile to the south. Three broad regions dominate its geography. A narrow coastal desert contains Lima and many other large cities. The Andes run north to south through the center, with deep valleys, high plateaus, and snowcapped peaks such as Huascarán. East of the mountains, the land descends into the vast Amazon Basin. The Marañón and Ucayali rivers join to form the upper Amazon, while Lake Titicaca lies high on the border with Bolivia.

The cold Humboldt Current helps make the coast unusually dry while supporting nutrient-rich waters and major fisheries. El Niño can warm the sea, disrupt fishing, and bring destructive coastal rain. Most people live along the coast or in Andean valleys, where roads, farms, and cities fit into limited usable land. Irrigation makes coastal agriculture possible, while highland farmers grow potatoes, quinoa, maize, and raise alpacas and llamas at different elevations. Copper, gold, and other minerals make mining economically important but place pressure on water supplies and mountain communities. The eastern rain forest is sparsely settled and exceptionally biodiverse. Earthquakes, landslides, floods, glacier loss, and difficult mountain travel strongly influence settlement and development.
