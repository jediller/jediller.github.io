Today's United States state is Mississippi.

Mississippi lies in the Deep South. Tennessee borders it on the north, Alabama on the east, Louisiana and the Gulf of Mexico on the south, and Louisiana and Arkansas across the Mississippi River on the west. Its land changes from the flat, fertile Yazoo Basin, commonly called the Delta, to loess bluffs, northern hills, longleaf-pine country, and a low Gulf Coast. The climate is humid subtropical, with hot, humid summers, mild winters, and roughly fifty to sixty-one inches of rain in a typical year. Hurricanes, river floods, and severe thunderstorms are recurring hazards.

The Mississippi River defines much of the western edge. The Yazoo, Big Black, Pearl, Pascagoula, and Tombigbee river systems drain the interior. Woodall Mountain, only eight hundred six feet high, is the state's highest point. Rich Delta soils helped make agriculture central to Mississippi history, while forests support timber and paper industries. Gulf ports, shipbuilding, seafood, and tourism connect the coast to the wider world. Jackson is the capital and largest city. Blues music grew from Black communities in the Delta and elsewhere, making Mississippi's landscape inseparable from a major American cultural tradition.

Today's country is The Bahamas.

The Bahamas is an Atlantic archipelago southeast of Florida and north of Cuba. It forms part of the Lucayan Archipelago and contains about seven hundred islands plus thousands of smaller cays, although only a small fraction are inhabited. The islands are exposed tops of broad limestone platforms called the Great and Little Bahama Banks. Most land is only a few feet above sea level. Mount Alvernia on Cat Island is the country's highest point at two hundred six feet.

Warm trade winds shape a tropical to subtropical climate, with a wetter summer season and a drier winter season. Hurricanes, storm surge, coastal erosion, and rising seas are major risks. The low limestone terrain has no large rivers, so communities depend heavily on fragile groundwater lenses, rainfall collection, and desalination. Shallow turquoise banks, coral reefs, mangroves, and seagrass beds support marine life and help protect shorelines.

Nassau, on New Providence Island, is the capital and the main population and commercial center. Freeport on Grand Bahama is another important port. Tourism and international financial services dominate the economy, with fishing, shipping, and construction also important. The country's dispersed islands make boats and aircraft essential for travel, supplies, health care, and disaster response.
