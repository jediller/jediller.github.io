Today's state is Montana.

Montana spans the northern Rocky Mountains and the western Great Plains. Canada lies to the north, with British Columbia, Alberta, and Saskatchewan along the border. Idaho is to the west, Wyoming to the south, and North Dakota and South Dakota to the east. The Continental Divide crosses the mountainous west, where Glacier National Park protects sharp peaks, glacial valleys, and lakes. East of the Rockies, broad plains, badlands, and isolated mountain ranges open toward the Dakotas.

Near Three Forks, the Jefferson, Madison, and Gallatin rivers join to form the Missouri River. The Yellowstone River crosses southern and eastern Montana, while Flathead Lake fills a large basin west of the divide. Montana has a dry continental climate. Winters can be severe, although warm Chinook winds sometimes raise temperatures quickly east of the mountains. Western valleys are generally wetter than the plains, and high elevations store winter snow that feeds rivers through spring and summer.

Mountain valleys and river corridors concentrate roads, towns, and farms. Wheat and cattle dominate much of the plains, while mining, timber, hydroelectric power, and energy production remain important in different regions. Tourism centers on Glacier and Yellowstone gateways, rivers, forests, and public lands. The long distances between communities and the rugged divide make transportation and public services challenging outside the larger cities.

Today's country is Angola.

Angola lies on the Atlantic coast of southwestern Africa. Namibia borders it to the south, Zambia to the east, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the north and northeast. The Cabinda exclave sits north of the main country, separated from it by a narrow strip of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. A narrow coastal plain rises steeply across an escarpment to the broad central highlands and the Bié Plateau. Morro de Moco is the country's highest point.

The Cuanza River flows west from the highlands to the Atlantic and supports water supply and hydroelectric power. Rivers rising in central Angola also feed the Congo and Zambezi systems. The Cubango flows southeast toward Botswana's Okavango Delta. Much of Angola has distinct wet and dry seasons. The cool Benguela Current limits rainfall along the coast, especially in the arid south, while the northern and central highlands receive more seasonal rain.

People are concentrated in the capital, Luanda, along the coast, and in the more temperate central highlands. Offshore oil is the leading export, and diamonds are mined mainly in the northeast. Fertile highland areas support cassava, maize, beans, coffee, and livestock. The plateau and escarpment complicate transport from the interior to ports, while drought in the south and rapid growth around Luanda place pressure on water, housing, and infrastructure.
