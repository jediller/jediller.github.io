Today is Tuesday, August eighteenth, two thousand twenty-six. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last twenty-four hours.

President Donald Trump told a Fox News reporter that the United States would bomb Oman if the Gulf ally got in the way of reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Fox News did not release audio of the interview. Trump also said the expired sixty-day deadline for a permanent settlement with Iran was irrelevant, said there was no timeline, and said the United States had a back channel to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Later in the Oval Office, he said he liked the idea of declaring the strait a United States territory, without explaining how that would work.

Discussing his order to reduce military exercises with South Korea, Trump said the United States could not protect countries that were unwilling to help it, and said all he wanted was fairness. He also said North Korean leader Kim Jong Un had responded to his request for a conversation, but provided no details. Trump separately dismissed concerns about mental health, supplies, and the record deployment aboard the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln as fake. He said an unnamed retired admiral had assured him that the ship was beautifully maintained and cared for.

Democratic Senator Tim Kaine said Trump was becoming increasingly deranged in what Kaine called a one-man campaign to threaten and initiate wars. Kaine said he would introduce a War Powers Resolution barring military action against Oman when the Senate returns from recess.

Senator Jack Reed, the ranking Democrat on the Senate Armed Services Committee, called Trump's reduction of the South Korea exercises an inane and haphazard decision. Reed said alliances depend on trust and strength, and argued that cutting exercises to flatter Kim or punish Seoul makes American commitments appear negotiable.

Customs and Border Protection Commissioner Rodney Scott announced that the agency was pausing construction activity in Big Bend National Park while he visits Texas for an on-the-ground evaluation. Scott said he would listen to local leaders, residents, and other stakeholders while weighing border security and protection of the park.
