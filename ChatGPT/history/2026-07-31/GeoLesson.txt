Today's United States state is Kansas.

Kansas lies near the geographic center of the contiguous United States. Nebraska borders it on the north, Missouri on the east, Oklahoma on the south, and Colorado on the west. The land generally rises from east to west. Wooded hills and tallgrass prairie in the east give way to broad plains and shorter grass farther west. Mount Sunflower, near the Colorado line, is the highest point at about four thousand thirty-nine feet, although it looks more like a gentle rise than a mountain.

The Kansas and Arkansas rivers cross the state, while the Republican and Smoky Hill rivers help drain the north and west. Kansas has a continental climate with hot summers, cold winters, strong winds, droughts, severe thunderstorms, and frequent tornado risk. Deep prairie soils and relatively flat land support large farms producing wheat, corn, sorghum, soybeans, and cattle. Irrigation from the High Plains Aquifer is important in western Kansas, but falling groundwater levels create a long-term challenge. Wichita is the largest city and a major aircraft-manufacturing center. Topeka is the capital. Kansas City's location near river and rail routes helped make the northeastern part of the state a transportation and logistics hub.

Today's country is Slovakia.

Slovakia is a landlocked country in Central Europe. It borders Poland to the north, Ukraine to the east, Hungary to the south, Austria to the southwest, and Czechia to the northwest. The Carpathian Mountains arc across the north, including the High Tatras, where Gerlachovský Peak rises to about eight thousand seven hundred eleven feet. Much of central Slovakia is mountainous and forested, while lower and warmer plains spread along the Danube River in the southwest and the East Slovak Lowland in the southeast.

The Danube forms part of the southern border and passes Bratislava, the capital. The Váh, Hron, and Hornád are other important rivers. Mountain snow and rainfall feed reservoirs and hydroelectric plants. The climate is temperate, with cold winters, warm summers, and cooler, wetter conditions at higher elevations. Fertile lowlands support wheat, corn, sunflowers, vineyards, and fruit, while forests cover much of the country.

Mountains historically separated valleys and encouraged distinct regional traditions. Today they support hiking, skiing, forestry, and tourism. Bratislava's position on the Danube, close to both Austria and Hungary, strengthens trade and industry. Western Slovakia has attracted automobile and electronics manufacturing, while eastern regions remain less densely industrialized. Railways and highways through mountain passes connect Slovakia with neighboring European markets.
