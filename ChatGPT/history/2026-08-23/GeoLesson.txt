United States State: Washington

Washington occupies the northwestern corner of the contiguous United States. Canada lies to the north, Idaho to the east, Oregon to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The Columbia River forms much of the Oregon boundary, while the Snake River drains the southeast. Puget Sound cuts deeply into the western lowlands and connects Seattle, Tacoma, islands, and sheltered ports to the sea. The Cascade Range divides the state. Moist Pacific air makes western Washington mild, rainy, and heavily forested, while the mountains create a rain shadow that leaves much of eastern Washington drier. Mount Rainier, a glacier-covered active volcano, is the state's highest peak. The Olympic Mountains rise west of Puget Sound, and the broad Columbia Plateau spreads east of the Cascades. Geography concentrates much of the population and trade around Puget Sound. Mountain snowpack feeds rivers used for drinking water, irrigation, and hydroelectric power. Fertile volcanic soils and irrigation support wheat, apples, hops, and vineyards east of the Cascades, while forests, fisheries, ports, aviation, and technology shape the west.

Sovereign Country: Belize

Belize lies on the Caribbean coast of Central America. Mexico borders it to the north, Guatemala to the west and south, and the Caribbean Sea to the east. The northern half is mostly low and flat, with coastal plains, wetlands, lagoons, and mangrove swamps. The land rises toward the Maya Mountains in the center and south, where forested ridges and limestone caves break the lowlands. Rivers include the Belize, Hondo, New, and Sarstoon. Offshore, hundreds of sandy or mangrove-covered cayes line the Belize Barrier Reef, part of the larger Mesoamerican Reef system. Belize has a tropical climate with distinct wet and dry seasons, and its coast is exposed to hurricanes, storm surge, flooding, and sea-level rise. Most people live in small coastal or river-linked settlements, while Belmopan, the inland capital, was developed after a hurricane devastated Belize City. Forests support conservation and some timber activity, fertile northern plains support sugar and citrus farming, and the reef, beaches, caves, and Maya sites make tourism central to the economy. The reef also protects the coast and supports fishing, so coral health matters directly to livelihoods.
