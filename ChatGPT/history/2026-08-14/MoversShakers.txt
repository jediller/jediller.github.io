Today is Friday, August fourteenth, two thousand twenty-six. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last twenty-four hours.

President Donald Trump used two appearances Friday to defend his handling of the war with Iran and to dismiss concerns about the long deployment of the aircraft carrier U.S.S. Abraham Lincoln. Asked about sailors who have spent more than two hundred forty uninterrupted days at sea, he said the deployment was not nearly long enough. He said the Lincoln was moving and would be replaced by a similar ship. Later, at a crime event in New York, Trump acknowledged that he had used the military beyond his original preference, but insisted the Iran operation was going well. He jokingly floated making the Strait of Hormuz a United States territory, refused to apologize for the war, and defended his decision.

Those remarks drew immediate responses from other American public figures. Representative Jason Crow, an Army veteran, accused the president of disregarding service members and their families. Senator Ruben Gallego, a Marine veteran, called reported conditions aboard the Lincoln not just disgusting but dangerous and said he was seeking a bipartisan oversight visit. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao separately posted that the Lincoln would return home soon. Their statements contrasted sharply with Trump's dismissal of the deployment's toll and kept attention on sailor welfare, military readiness, and the unresolved course of the Iran conflict.
