Today is Saturday, August fifteenth, two thousand twenty-six. Here is what the movers and shakers are noted as saying in the last twenty-four hours.

At a law-and-order rally on Long Island, Donald Trump praised new federal statistics showing violent crime declined in twenty twenty-five, attacked Democratic leadership, and promoted Bruce Blakeman and other New York Republican candidates. In the same appearance, Trump urged Americans to accept higher gasoline prices as the cost of confronting Iran and said he might declare the Strait of Hormuz United States territory. Later, when reporters asked about families' concerns over the U.S.S. Abraham Lincoln's record Middle East deployment, he rejected those concerns and said the deployment was not nearly long enough.

Vice President J. D. Vance asked the Justice Department to investigate hospitals named in a new Health and Human Services report. His letter alleged that some institutions may have used misleading or fraudulent billing codes for gender-affirming care, and requested an inquiry into dozens of hospitals. That was a direct request from Vance, not merely reporting about administration policy.

On his podcast, Joe Rogan criticized the Trump family's cryptocurrency business and the paid Truth Social service that gives subscribers early access to market-moving presidential posts. Rogan called the government's crypto conduct nuts and said Trump had made billions from crypto. He reacted with disbelief when the early-access arrangement was described to him.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said in a public speech that restoring federal voting-rights protections and pursuing major Supreme Court reforms would be top priorities if Democrats regain governing power.

Florida Governor Ron DeSantis stated plainly that he had not endorsed Representative Cory Mills and did not support him in Tuesday's Republican primary. DeSantis made the statement while appearing at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University.

After five people were killed in Missaukee County, Michigan, Governor Gretchen Whitmer said she had been briefed, was monitoring the situation, and was thinking of the victims, their families, and the community.
