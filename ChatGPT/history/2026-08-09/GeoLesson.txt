New Jersey lies on the Atlantic coast between New York and Pennsylvania. The Delaware River and Delaware Bay form most of its western boundary, while the Hudson River and Upper New York Bay edge the northeast. The Atlantic Ocean borders the east and south. Northern New Jersey includes the forested Highlands and the Kittatinny Ridge, where High Point is the state's highest elevation. South and east of the Highlands, the Piedmont contains rolling terrain and many of the largest cities. The broad Coastal Plain covers most of southern New Jersey, including the sandy Pine Barrens, tidal marshes, barrier islands, and beaches of the Jersey Shore.

The Passaic, Hackensack, and Raritan rivers drain toward New York Harbor and the Atlantic, while western streams flow to the Delaware. New Jersey's position between New York City and Philadelphia encouraged dense settlement, ports, railways, highways, warehousing, and manufacturing. Newark and Jersey City grew beside New York Harbor, while Camden developed across the Delaware from Philadelphia. Fertile Coastal Plain soils support vegetables, blueberries, cranberries, and nursery crops, giving the state its Garden State nickname. Summers are warm and humid, winters are colder inland, and the coast moderates temperature but faces hurricanes, nor'easters, erosion, and flooding. Highlands forests supply drinking water to much of the state's population.
