United States State: Oregon

Oregon occupies the Pacific Northwest between Washington, Idaho, Nevada, California, and the Pacific Ocean. The north-flowing Snake River marks much of its eastern boundary, and the west-flowing Columbia River forms most of the border with Washington before reaching the sea. The Coast Range rises behind a rugged shoreline. Farther inland, the volcanic Cascade Range divides the wet west from the drier interior and contains Mount Hood, Oregon's highest point. West of the Cascades, the Willamette Valley has mild, rainy winters, dry summers, fertile soils, and most of the state's population, including Portland, Salem, and Eugene. East of the mountains, high desert, broad basins, lava fields, and the Blue and Wallowa mountains experience greater temperature extremes and much less rain. The Columbia gorge creates a natural transportation corridor through the Cascades. Forests support timber and recreation, rivers provide irrigation and hydropower, and the Willamette Valley produces berries, wine grapes, grass seed, and nursery crops. Coastal ports, fishing, technology, and tourism also reflect Oregon's varied geography.

Sovereign Country: Portugal

Portugal lies on the western edge of the Iberian Peninsula, bordered by Spain to the north and east and the Atlantic Ocean to the west and south. The country also includes the volcanic Atlantic archipelagos of the Azores and Madeira. Northern mainland Portugal is generally hillier, cooler, and wetter, while the south has broader plains, hotter summers, and a Mediterranean climate. The Tagus River crosses the center and reaches the ocean at Lisbon's wide estuary. The Douro drains the north through a steep valley famous for vineyards, and the Guadiana helps form part of the Spanish border. The Serra da Estrela contains the mainland's highest mountains. Lisbon and Porto grew around sheltered river mouths that encouraged maritime trade, fishing, shipbuilding, and overseas connections. Fertile valleys support grapes, olives, cork oak, citrus, and grains, while dry southern areas depend heavily on reservoirs and irrigation. A long coastline supports ports, tourism, and renewable energy, but drought, wildfire, coastal erosion, and earthquake risk shape planning and settlement.
