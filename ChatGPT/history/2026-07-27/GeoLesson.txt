Today's state is West Virginia.

West Virginia lies entirely within the Appalachian region and is the only state located wholly within Appalachia. It borders Pennsylvania and Maryland to the north and east, Virginia to the southeast, Kentucky to the southwest, and Ohio across the Ohio River to the northwest. Parallel ridges and valleys shape the east, while the Allegheny Plateau covers much of the center and west with steep, forested hills cut by streams. Spruce Knob, at 4,863 feet, is the state's highest point. The New, Gauley, Kanawha, Monongahela, and Potomac river systems drain its rugged terrain. The New River Gorge cuts deeply through ancient rock and is now protected as a national park. Narrow valleys concentrate many towns, roads, and rail lines, while abundant forests support recreation and timber. Coal seams helped create mining communities and heavy industry, though steep slopes and flash-flood risk make construction and transportation difficult.

Today's country is Micronesia, formally the Federated States of Micronesia.

The Federated States of Micronesia is a Pacific island country spread across the western Caroline Islands, north of Papua New Guinea and east of Palau. Its four states are Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, and Kosrae. Together they contain more than 600 islands scattered across an ocean area far larger than their land area. Some islands are high volcanic peaks with rain forests, waterfalls, and fertile valleys. Others are low coral atolls that rise only a few feet above sea level. Pohnpei's Mount Nanlaud is the country's highest point. Warm tropical seas, coral reefs, lagoons, and mangroves support fishing and protect shorelines. Heavy rainfall is common on the high islands, while typhoons can damage crops and homes. Settlements cluster along coasts because steep interiors limit building. Great distances between islands make transport and public services costly, and sea-level rise threatens freshwater supplies and low-lying communities.
