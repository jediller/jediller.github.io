United States State: South Dakota

South Dakota lies in the north-central United States. North Dakota is to the north, Minnesota and Iowa to the east, Nebraska to the south, and Wyoming and Montana to the west. The Missouri River crosses the state from north to south and is impounded by several large dams, creating long reservoirs that provide electricity, irrigation, recreation, and flood control. East of the river, rolling prairie and glacial plains contain fertile soils and many small lakes. West of the river, the land becomes drier and rises through broad grasslands toward the Black Hills. Black Elk Peak is the state's highest point. The Badlands expose sharply eroded layers of clay and sediment in ridges, buttes, and gullies. South Dakota has a continental climate with cold winters, hot summers, strong winds, drought risk, and severe thunderstorms. Farming and most larger towns are concentrated in the wetter east, where corn, soybeans, and livestock are important. Cattle ranching dominates much of the western prairie. The Missouri River is both a geographic divide and a transportation challenge, while the Black Hills support forestry, tourism, mining, and rapidly growing communities around Rapid City.

Sovereign Country: Tonga

Tonga is a Polynesian island country in the South Pacific, northeast of New Zealand and east of Fiji. Its islands form a long north-to-south chain between the open Pacific and the deep Tonga Trench. The eastern islands are mostly low platforms of coral limestone, while many western islands are volcanic and rise more steeply from the sea. Active undersea volcanoes, earthquakes, and tsunamis reflect the meeting of tectonic plates along this part of the Pacific Ring of Fire. The capital, Nuku'alofa, is on low-lying Tongatapu, the country's largest and most populated island. Tonga has a warm tropical ocean climate, with a wetter, hotter season and a cooler, drier season. Tropical cyclones, coastal flooding, saltwater intrusion, and sea-level rise threaten settlements, roads, crops, and freshwater supplies. Limited land and long distances between island groups make transport and imported goods costly. Fertile volcanic soils and warm conditions support root crops, coconuts, bananas, and squash, while surrounding waters support fishing. Most settlement is coastal, and reefs help protect shorelines and attract visitors. Geography also encourages overseas migration, making remittances from Tongans abroad important to family and national life.
