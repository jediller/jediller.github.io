Today's state is Pennsylvania.

Pennsylvania stretches from the Delaware River in the east to a short shoreline on Lake Erie in the northwest. New York lies to the north, New Jersey to the east, Delaware and Maryland to the south, West Virginia to the southwest, and Ohio to the west. The Appalachian Mountains cross the state from southwest to northeast in long forested ridges and valleys. The Allegheny Plateau covers much of the west and north, while lower rolling country surrounds Philadelphia in the southeast.

The Susquehanna River drains a large central basin into Chesapeake Bay. In the west, the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers meet at Pittsburgh to form the Ohio River. The Delaware River helped make Philadelphia a major port and marks much of the eastern boundary. Pennsylvania has a humid climate with four distinct seasons; higher elevations are cooler and snowier, especially near Lake Erie.

Mountains historically complicated east-west travel, but river valleys and later canals, railroads, and turnpikes tied the interior to coastal markets. Coal and iron supported early industry, and natural gas remains important. Fertile limestone valleys support dairy farms, corn, mushrooms, and fruit. Today, most people live around Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and smaller valley cities, while forests, state parks, and the Pocono Mountains support recreation and tourism.

Today's country is Morocco.

Morocco occupies the northwest corner of Africa, facing both the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea. The Strait of Gibraltar separates its northern coast from Spain. Algeria lies to the east. To the south is Western Sahara, most of which Morocco administers, although its sovereignty remains internationally disputed. The Rif Mountains rise near the Mediterranean, and the Middle, High, and Anti-Atlas ranges cross the interior. Jebel Toubkal in the High Atlas is North Africa's highest peak, at about thirteen thousand six hundred seventy-one feet.

The Sebou and Oum Er-Rbia rivers carry water from the mountains toward fertile Atlantic plains. Mediterranean conditions bring mild, wetter winters and hot, dry summers to the north and west. The Atlas ranges capture rain and snow, but the land becomes much drier toward the Sahara in the southeast. Drought and uneven rainfall make reservoirs and irrigation essential.

Most Moroccans live in the coastal plains and northern cities, including Casablanca, Rabat, Tangier, and Fez. Productive lowlands grow wheat, olives, citrus fruit, and vegetables. Atlantic fisheries, large phosphate deposits, manufacturing, tourism, and major ports also support the economy. Mountain passes channel roads and settlements, while Morocco's position beside the Strait of Gibraltar gives it strategic access to shipping routes between the Atlantic and Mediterranean.
