United States State: Oklahoma

Oklahoma lies in the south-central United States, where the Great Plains meet the wooded uplands of the Southeast. It borders Colorado and Kansas on the north, Missouri and Arkansas on the east, Texas on the south and west, and New Mexico at the western tip of its Panhandle. The Red River forms much of the southern boundary, while the Arkansas River and tributaries such as the Canadian and Cimarron cross the state. Elevation and rainfall generally decrease toward the west. The humid east includes the Ozark and Ouachita uplands, forests, and many reservoirs. Central Oklahoma has rolling prairie and the Cross Timbers, and the west rises into drier High Plains, mesas, and the Wichita Mountains. Oklahoma's location between cold continental air and warm Gulf air brings hot summers, variable winters, drought, severe thunderstorms, and frequent tornadoes. Most people live around Oklahoma City and Tulsa, where rivers, highways, railways, energy development, and broad plains aided growth. Eastern and central farms produce cattle, wheat, poultry, and soybeans, while oil, natural gas, and wind power are especially important across the plains.

Sovereign Country: Georgia

Georgia is a country in the South Caucasus, between the Black Sea and the crest of the Greater Caucasus Mountains. It borders Russia to the north, Azerbaijan to the east and southeast, Armenia to the south, and Türkiye to the southwest. The Greater Caucasus contains high glaciated peaks, including Shkhara, while the Lesser Caucasus and volcanic uplands shape the south. The Mtkvari, or Kura, River drains eastern Georgia toward the Caspian Sea, and the Rioni flows west to the Black Sea. Moist sea air makes western lowlands and mountain slopes lush and rainy; eastern valleys are drier and more continental. Mountain barriers create sharp local climate differences and make some communities vulnerable to landslides, avalanches, and earthquakes. Tbilisi grew in the Mtkvari valley at a natural crossroads, while Kutaisi occupies the Rioni basin and Batumi is a Black Sea port. Fertile valleys support grapes, fruit, tea, and hazelnuts. Steep rivers provide hydropower, mountain scenery supports tourism, and Georgia's position between Europe and Asia makes roads, railways, pipelines, and Black Sea ports important to trade.
