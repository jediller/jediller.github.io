Today's United States state is Nevada.

Nevada lies in the interior West. It borders Oregon and Idaho on the north, Utah and Arizona on the east, and California on the west and southwest. Most of the state belongs to the Basin and Range Province, where long, narrow mountain ranges alternate with broad desert valleys. Nevada has hundreds of named ranges and is the nation's most mountainous state by that measure. The Sierra Nevada rises along part of the western edge, while the Colorado River and Lake Mead help define the southeastern boundary.

Most of Nevada is dry because the Sierra Nevada blocks much Pacific moisture. The north has cold-desert conditions, while the Mojave Desert around Las Vegas is hotter. Water strongly shapes settlement. Las Vegas relies heavily on the Colorado River, and Reno and Carson City grew near the better-watered eastern foot of the Sierra Nevada. Federal land covers most of the state, leaving population and development concentrated in a few valleys. Mining, especially for gold, remains important, while tourism and entertainment dominate Las Vegas and Reno. Carson City is the capital, and Las Vegas is the largest city.

Denmark occupies the Jutland Peninsula and more than four hundred named islands between the North Sea and Baltic Sea. Its only land border is with Germany. The largest islands include Zealand, where Copenhagen is located, and Funen. Denmark is low and gently rolling because glaciers shaped the landscape. No point reaches six hundred feet above sea level, and long coastlines, shallow bays, sandy beaches, and fertile plains are characteristic.

The surrounding seas give Denmark a mild maritime climate with cool summers, relatively moderate winters, frequent wind, and rainfall through the year. No place in the country is far from the coast. Fertile soils support grain, dairy, and livestock farming, while excellent harbors encouraged fishing, shipping, trade, and the growth of Copenhagen. Strong winds and shallow coastal waters also favor wind-energy development, including offshore turbines. Bridges and tunnels link major islands and connect Copenhagen with Sweden across the Øresund.
